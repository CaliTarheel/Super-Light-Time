# Scientific basis and interpretation

> **Documentation scope:** This retained design document includes historical development results. Refer to [KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md](KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md) and [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md) for the initial GitHub snapshot. References to local `validation/`, `output/`, legacy copies, and handoff documents describe artifacts not included in this repository.

This project is a reduced-complexity forward model for generating and reviewing plausible tectonic worlds. Its 1,000 Myr history is one conditional scenario. It is not a prediction of Earth's future, a reconstruction of an unknown real planet, or a three-dimensional mantle-convection calculation. Changing the initial plate arrangement, seed, or process coefficients produces a different history.

New histories carry four additional foundations: explicit material crustal columns with Airy-style buoyancy and erosion rebound, rift cooling and foreland accommodation, local coherent terrane accretion, and persistent subduction-system histories. [FOUNDATIONS.md](FOUNDATIONS.md) documents their fields, coefficients and height accounting. Earlier saved histories retain their original equations and schema; these additions do not revise old worlds or their source records.

The user has adopted **C. R. Scotese, _Plate Tectonics: Rules of Thumb_, updated 13 August 2012** as a guiding document for Deep Time's tectonic design. [RULES-OF-THUMB.md](RULES-OF-THUMB.md) preserves the complete supplied edition, with source provenance. The application principles below guide future physics and initialization changes; the [corrections and limits](#corrections-and-limits-to-the-supplied-rules-of-thumb) distinguish useful heuristics from numerical errors and claims that are not universal laws. The source's geological imperatives are reference material, not commands to execute or permission to change a running world.

## Applying the guiding document

**C. R. Scotese is the patron saint of Deep Time.** Decisions should be physically accurate and consistent with his views on tectonics wherever possible. When they conflict, physical accuracy takes priority and the departure must be noted. For each departure, identify the relevant rule or view, describe the chosen behavior, explain the reason with supporting evidence where available, and state the consequences or limitations. Record this in the relevant design documentation and reference it in the change description. Distinguish a necessary computational approximation from a scientific disagreement; neither should be presented as Scotese's position. The preserved PDF edition is the primary reference for his views here; attribute additional views to a specific source.

The design goal is an understandable chain from plate geometry and inherited geology to forces, motion, and resulting geography. Each moving continent should have an explanation that can be shown on the map and checked in the force budget. These are design requirements for future changes, not a claim that every relationship in the source is already simulated.

### Historical same-sheet geometry at a source transition

The 652 Ma checkpoint of the first long-running world predates the exact same-sheet nonpenetration guard. A direct geometry audit found 201 overlapping face pairs on the same collision sheet, with 87,673 km² of summed pairwise overlap. Some face pairs may cover the same ground, so this sum is not a unique land-area estimate. Clamping all vertices of these already-intersecting faces cannot restore a nonoverlapping mesh: a trial continuation hit the guard's unresolved-overlap rejection even for a 0.001 Myr step. The historical checkpoint therefore retains guard version 0 when opened by newer source, and diagnostics explicitly leave its final overlap count unevaluated. New simulations initialize guard version 1 and must pass the zero-overlap postcondition before committing a step.

This is a compatibility boundary for an existing numerical history, not a physically justified intersection or a repair. It departs from the intent of Scotese's Rule XI (continental collisions matter) because the historical mesh can represent overlapping continental sheets. Its consequence is that geography and collision forces after this checkpoint still need scrutiny; a fully physical rewind or mesh repair requires a separate validated reconstruction. No force coefficient is changed to conceal the overlap.

The [ridge-push and opposing-subduction review](RIDGE-AND-OPPOSING-SLAB-REVIEW.md) records the installed ridge-force scale, saved-world diagnostics, related PR work, and the missing path from mutually canceling slab forces to internal tensile deformation. It also distinguishes current native ocean breakup from the historical original-ocean protection described later in this document.

1. **Make attached slabs and gravitational ridge forces the primary explanation for plate motion.** An inherited sinking slab supplies negative-buoyancy pull to its attached plate; lithospheric gravitational potential supplies ridge push. Balance these against bending, interface resistance, and mantle resistance. Diagnose slow motion through the actual torques and plate connections before changing coefficients. Do not add an imposed mantle conveyor or arbitrary velocity kick simply to make continents move.
2. **Distinguish continents from plates.** A plate can carry both continental and oceanic lithosphere. A continent can move because ocean floor belonging to that same plate is subducting far offshore. Preserve the starting continent and craton geometry while making plate ownership, ocean connections, and subduction polarity explicit. A coastline need not be a plate boundary.
3. **Let the geometry determine the net torque.** More trench length does not necessarily mean faster motion: opposing pulls can cancel. Declare finite, asymmetric subduction systems where the initial geology calls for them. Record inherited slab geometry and inventory as initial conditions; do not infer existing slabs merely from a newly colored boundary.
4. **Make spreading and rifting respond to extension.** A ridge is a site of lithosphere production where plates separate, while its thermal structure can contribute gravitational ridge push. Moving a trench must not reset old ocean floor to ridge age. Retain the provenance of inherited ridges, cooling ages, sutures, and weak regions; later breakup needs a stated loading and weakening mechanism.
5. **Carry subduction and collision history forward.** Track which plate is incoming, which overrides, and what oceanic lithosphere remains attached. Ridge consumption, buoyant continental arrival, accretion, and termination should have understandable consequences. Do not let numerical boundary relabeling silently reverse polarity or erase a slab. Scotese's persistent-subduction picture is a useful organizing principle; exceptional shutdown mechanisms still need explicit treatment where implemented.
6. **Treat plumes and inherited weakness as modifiers.** Weakening and gravitational uplift may help breakup, but the baseline design should explain sustained plate motion without requiring a hot spot under every continent. Keep any such forcing and its energy contribution visible.
7. **Make the causal explanation reviewable.** Save plate connections and trench polarity, driving and resisting torque contributions, local speed in cm/year, and the initial-condition choices needed to reproduce them. Use actual continental displacement, force balance, passive resisting work, conservation budgets, and restart agreement to assess changes. A visually active map alone does not establish that the mechanism works.

This adopts the slab-driven emphasis of the supplied rules without claiming that real mantle flow has no dynamical role. Slabs, plates, and the mantle are coupled; slab-induced flow can exert traction on other plates. Any future representation of that coupling should identify its physical source and budget rather than prescribe motion to conceal missing plate connectivity. [Conrad and Lithgow-Bertelloni, 2002](https://www.clintconrad.no/papers/Conrad_Science2002.pdf)

### Proposed slab-driven startup

The [Lite effective subduction law](EFFECTIVE-SUBDUCTION.md) offers a simpler alternative: constant horizontal force per metre of declared finite trench acts on the incoming plate, and the existing balance solves motion from rest without creating a slab inventory or velocity kick. Quiet motion does not switch off a declared trace; force stops locally on fully buoyant incoming material and the existing collision path handles arrival. This is an explicit approximation of Rules I–II, IV and VI, with reaction and power assigned to an omitted slab/mantle reservoir. It does not resolve slab buoyancy, initiation, breakoff, upper-plate suction or independent rollback, and it preserves the need for correct ocean–continent ownership. Lite is the default for new reviewed worlds in this fork; detailed laws remain selectable and saved worlds retain their recorded laws. Map and material detail remain unchanged. See the experiment document for configuration, shared force-ledger rifting, measured runtime evidence and its limits.

The reviewed ocean-splitting startup assigns independent ocean plates to the water between separate continental plates. At its seeded coastal trenches, those ocean plates are incoming and receive direct slab pull; the continental plates override them. Stronger slab pull therefore primarily speeds the oceans. Merely shifting a trench offshore with the same ownership and polarity does not give the continental plate an attached sinking slab.

A useful next design is to join a connected ocean region to an adjacent continental plate, keeping the land and its material intact. That coast becomes an internal, passive margin. Where the far side of the attached oceanic region subducts beneath another plate, its slab pulls the mixed plate, including the continent. Selected regions can be attached explicitly while other ocean plates remain independent. The existing ridge geometry and cooling ages should be retained wherever the revised boundary arrangement permits them; incompatible assignments must be rejected or explicitly redesigned. This is a proposed initialization change requiring implementation and fresh-run validation.

An alternative offshore arrangement gives a selected continental plate a connected oceanic extension and places a finite trench arc at its seaward edge, with that oceanic extension on the incoming side. The other side must belong to a distinct overriding plate. An equally loaded trench around an entire symmetric oceanic rim can cancel its own net torque, so a uniform ring is not a general solution. Trench position, polarity, extent, and slab inventory are declared geological assumptions; moving a trench does not create young crust.

A third, symmetric arrangement is now available as an explicit experiment: [a declared ring of subduction around one supercontinent plate](GREAT-CIRCLE-STARTUP.md), whose own oceanic skirt sinks beneath the distant ocean on every side. Its pull cancels in the rigid balance by design, so the question becomes which part of the plate fails in tension. A read-only force-based limit analysis finds that, with an old skirt, the continent tears first (Rule III), while a young skirt at the trench tears the ocean first. The current engine does not act on that tension: its rift laws read only relative velocity, and its trench shutdown law switches the stationary ring off after 10–20 Myr, in conflict with Rule IV. The design document records these results, the departures from Pangea's actual setting, and the proposed coupling.

For the attached-region or offshore-arc arrangement, demonstrate that the intended continent receives nonzero slab torque through its attached oceanic lithosphere with unchanged force coefficients, that its land geometry and material are preserved at initialization, and that buoyant continental arrival is handled without consuming it as ocean floor. A short evolved preflight must establish displacement, budget closure, and checkpoint reproducibility before adopting a fresh production run. The explanation should be as concrete as: **this continent is attached to this ocean floor, whose far edge is sinking at this trench.**

## What a starting map specifies

The opt-in [surface erosion coupling](SURFACE-EROSION-COUPLING.md) drives
subaerial erosion from the shared exposed coastal surface and preserves the
existing conservative column transaction. Its finite spherical quadrature and
face-average removal are explicit numerical approximations.

The opt-in [collision coast reconstruction](COLLISION-COAST-RECONSTRUCTION.md)
distinguishes exposed material-union coastlines from buried sheet edges. It
preserves mechanical inventories and historical frames; its finite-toe blending
is an explicit surface approximation, not a new collision pressure solve.

A continent is a region of relatively buoyant continental crust. A craton is an old, relatively strong continental interior. A tectonic plate is a moving region of lithosphere and can include continental crust, oceanic crust, or both. Coastlines, crustal edges, craton edges, and active plate boundaries are different things.

An arbitrary map of crust and cratons alone does not determine motion. A physical initial-value problem additionally needs lithospheric temperature and composition, inherited faults, plate boundaries and velocities, existing slabs, mantle temperature and viscosity, and surface-process conditions. Here, missing information is supplied by seeded initial conditions and model parameters. Starting with a Pangaea-like supercontinent also requires an assumed mechanism and direction of initial extension; its outline cannot uniquely determine how it will break up.

All times in the application are **elapsed Myr since the starting world**. `0` is the initial condition and `1000` is one billion years later. They are not dates in Earth's geological timescale. Negative height denotes terrain below the model's selected sea-level datum.

The optional **Land-rich** starting world is a seeded, lobed spherical supercontinent with **65% continental crust including 18 separated cratons**. Coverage is selected using exact latitude-cell areas, to within one raster cell of the target. It specifies an intentionally continental worldbuilding scenario, not an estimate of Earth's crustal area or a prediction that 65% will remain above sea level. The classic starting world remains available. Spherical brushes use angular distance, and the editor's fill operation uses longitude wrapping and reflected polar adjacency; changing grid size resamples material classes without evolving geology.

## Established relationships and the approximations they allow

| Process | Scientific basis | Appropriate interpretation in this model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plate motion | A rigid displacement on a sphere is an Euler rotation. Instantaneous velocity is `v = omega cross r`; it varies with distance from the rotation pole. [GPlates](https://www.gplates.org/docs/user-manual/reconstructions/) | Spherical rotation is sound kinematics. A generated Euler pole or a rule for changing angular velocity is an assumption about dynamics, not a uniquely inferred force balance. |
| Driving and resisting forces | Negative buoyancy of attached slabs supplies slab pull, with resistance from bending, interfaces, and mantle interaction. Slab pull can exceed ridge push substantially. [Spence, 1987](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/slab-pull-and-seismotectonics-subducting-lithosphere) | Boundary-dependent force or torque terms capture feedback between subduction and motion. Their coefficients and prescribed background forcing are effective parameters; there is no universal 80/20 force partition. |
| Rifting and cratons | Inherited thermal and rheological structure changes strain localization, rift geometry, and breakup style. [Rheological inheritance study, 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-24945-5) | Stronger cratons and weaker sutures are useful parameterizations. Scalar strength and splitting criteria cannot resolve brittle faults, ductile flow, magmatic weakening, or the geometry of individual rift basins. |
| Ocean cooling | Oceanic lithosphere cools and subsides with age; plate-cooling models fit age, depth, and heat flow jointly. [Stein and Stein, 1992](https://www.nature.com/articles/359123a0) | An age-depth relation supplies broad bathymetry. Its height is a background thermal signal, before trench relief, volcanic construction, sediments, and any explicit sea-level offset. It does not imply that arbitrary ocean crust can remain physically stable indefinitely. |
| Subduction, arcs, and accretion | Convergent margins include forearc, magmatic arc, and back-arc regions. Buoyant terrane accretion can increase coupling; advancing and retreating systems have different deformation styles. [Cawood et al., 2009](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/accretionary-orogens-through-earth-history) | Arc construction, transfer of buoyant crust to an overriding plate, shortening, and crustal thickening can be tracked as effective surface processes. They are not petrological melt production or an explicit slab cross-section. |
| Subduction initiation and polarity | Initiation, episodic restart, polarity reversal and simple trench separation describe different histories. [Crameri et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7385650/) | Local trench identities retain polarity and observed convergence; heuristic maturation and shutdown delays regulate arc supply. They do not solve slab depth, initiation mechanics or a self-consistent subduction force balance. |
| Topography and erosion | Crustal thickness and overlying loads affect local Airy compensation. [Harmonica Airy equations](https://www.fatiando.org/harmonica/latest/api/generated/harmonica.isostatic_moho_airy.html) | Material columns now carry explicit thickness, water-loaded buoyancy and denudation with rebound. Fixed densities, instantaneous local compensation and a simple denudation request remain approximations. Raster overlap supplies no separate elevation bonus. |
| Continental basins | Lithospheric stretching thins crust; cooling after extension produces further subsidence. [McKenzie, 1978](https://www.zetaware.com/public/McKenzie_1978.pdf) | Material-attached thermal support decays while thinned crust persists. A separate finite foreland-depression proxy represents accommodation near current mountain loads. Neither rule solves sediment infill or an elastic lithosphere. |
| Full geodynamics | Mantle models couple momentum and mass conservation to temperature and composition; material properties and boundary/initial conditions determine the solution. [ASPECT basic equations](https://aspect-documentation.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/methods/basic-equations/index.html) | The application does not solve these coupled three-dimensional equations. Its process rules make laptop-scale exploration practical while limiting quantitative accuracy. |

### Units and useful independent checks

The exact speed conversion is:

```text
1 cm/year  = 10 km/Myr
10 cm/year = 100 km/Myr
```

On a sphere of radius 6,371 km, a one-degree great-circle arc is approximately 111.195 km. Thus 10 cm/year corresponds to approximately **0.8993 degrees/Myr** along a great circle, not exactly one degree/Myr. A plate's angular speed about its Euler pole is not its local surface speed divided by planet radius except on the pole's equator.

For the fixed-density material columns now used in new histories, local Airy compensation converts a change in total crustal thickness `delta_T` to subaerial elevation by:

```text
delta_h = delta_T * (mantle_density - crust_density) / mantle_density
```

The implemented densities of 3,300 and 2,800 kg/m3 give about 1.52 km of surface rise for 10 km of added crust; the rest deepens the crustal base. This follows equal mass above a compensation depth. Water-covered columns include a 1,030 kg/m3 water load. Elastic or dynamic support can invalidate this local-column assumption. The saved `crustal_root_km` is positive thickness excess over a column's neutral reference, not an absolute Moho depth or a separately calculated basal-root thickness.

### What the implementation actually carries

Continental crust and cratons are represented by persistent moving parcels. Their weights are **reference area in km2**, not rock mass in kilograms. Sustained local convergence can transfer a connected buoyant terrane and its supporting membership to a receiver. Contact memory follows actual nearby material patches, so remote islands sharing the source plate do not inherit the same collision clock. Whole patches and protected cratons remain coherent; the source retains its remote material and oceanic lithosphere. Accreted juvenile arcs retain their origin history and column state when their current class becomes continental. Rendering parcels onto the geographic grid remains a sampling operation; thresholded map area need not equal the sum of weights.

Each parcel and marker now carries thickness, neutral thickness/elevation, effective area, rift thermal support and cooling age, plus foreland deflection. Mechanical thickening/thinning conserves `thickness × effective area`; magma adds column material and denudation removes it. Effective area does not resize the transported footprint. The original retained-mass diagnostic therefore still checks reference-area retention, not full three-dimensional conservation or shape preservation. Juvenile coalescence preserves weighted full height and effective column volume. Grid deposition averages full material elevations before display classification, preventing a class change from creating an artificial height jump.

Each buoyant parcel now carries an oriented, anisotropic finite footprint. Its tangent orientation rotates with the same Euler motion as its position. Deposition approximately integrates the parcel and destination-cell footprints, then normalizes contributions to the parcel's exact reference area. A footprint that includes a pole can contribute around the corresponding polar ring; north and south caps are not connected. This replaces point-only longitude/latitude splatting that left artificial holes when material crossed a pole. It is a conservative numerical remap, not new crust production or morphological hole filling. Finite footprints still smooth sampling near shorelines, with uncertainty of roughly one source cell; they are not deforming finite elements or a solution of crustal strain. Unresolved footprints have a local interpolation fallback, and coalesced juvenile arcs receive a fresh local footprint.

Finite-footprint remapping did not solve a separate failure in later fracture decisions. The former classifier could send the four samples of one original crust cell to different plates, and surface-only craton checks could miss protected material concealed by overlapping crust. Different Euler motions then separated those samples while conserving every weight. The current representation gives each original cell an enduring `parcel_patch` identity and each original connected craton a `parcel_craton` identity. Fractures classify an area's spherical patch centroid and apply that one result to all its samples; any cut dividing a protected material craton is rejected even when the surface raster hides it. Associated history markers follow their actual patch. This imposes coherent rigid motion within the chosen material units; it does not calculate elastic or ductile strain, and craton protection remains an explicit worldbuilding choice.

Ocean plate coverage is transported using continuous per-plate membership fields with bilinear interpolation. This accumulates motions smaller than a raster cell instead of rounding each step to zero. Ocean age and relief are also interpolated. This method introduces numerical diffusion, especially across narrow plates and age discontinuities. Rigid Euler motion of each plate does not imply exact advection or exact spherical area conservation of its raster footprint.

Active ocean spreading uses a finite half-stage rotation between the neighboring plates to back-sample **one shared fractional interface field per pair**. Independent planes fitted to individual faces could disagree and generate alternating ownership stripes; the shared field preserves a coherent curved transition while allowing subcell motion. Finite-stage shore separation supplies the opening rate and paired new-crust strips. A stationary ridge axis can therefore continue spreading. The mapped fractional interface is still interpolated on a raster: its zero crossing is not an exact analytic finite-rotation axis. In the current 80 Myr asymmetric-opening fixture at widths 64, 128 and 256, the position error stays below half a cell and decreases with refinement. This is a bounded numerical check, not a guarantee for all geometries or a global convergence result.

Paired strips receive equal realized reference area after local junction restrictions and cell-capacity limits. Existing third-plate territory and genuinely arriving third-plate material block local reconstruction. These guards do not globally prune legitimate off-ridge fractional transport. Saved opening and axis-drift diagnostics describe the last integration step; mean speeds are segment-length weighted. Coarse cells retain mixed mean ocean ages rather than individual magnetic-age bands. The front cache remains a local representation, not a complete evolving plate-boundary graph. Older saved histories keep their recorded geometry and counters, and the mantle-driving formulas remain unchanged.

Ocean-created, ocean-consumed, and accreted-area counters accumulate process activity over time. They can count repeated coverage changes or transfers of the same material. They are not a closed global mass budget or the area of uniquely identified rock produced or destroyed. Arc-added reference area is tracked separately so the original buoyant-material retention diagnostic does not mistake newly added arc material for preserved starting material.

The starting continental partition uses recursive continuous spherical curves with seeded bends at several angular scales. A periodic shape-preserving cubic interpolant gives broad curvature and smaller meanders, with rounded detours around connected cratonic bounding regions. It replaces the earlier linear segments and straight-sided bypass shelves. Curvature remains a geometric worldbuilding rule, not a fracture-mechanics solution. The same continuous classifier assigns raster cells and whole material patches, so fine curve samples do not become independently moving fragments. Curves retain continuous tangents through their nodes and periodic seam; their evaluation is chunked to bound temporary memory.

The supplied crust painting is preserved while assigning continental plate ownership. Starting cuts are rejected if they divide a mapped craton, create excessive component fragmentation, or divide a candidate block too unevenly. The requested plate count is only a target: an all-craton world or a world lacking safe corridors may produce fewer blocks. Progressive later continental cuts use developed mechanical components to assign whole material patches, with markers following their patches. A spherical side classifier supplies the ocean continuation. Connectivity validation permits inherited islands and bounds newly disconnected scraps; the separate full-domain check records resulting visible-domain identities.

Plate connectivity includes oceanic lithosphere as well as continents. The domain check uses connected raster cells with longitude wrapping and same-cap continuation over each pole. Disconnected landmasses can correctly share one domain if their oceanic lithosphere connects them. Each disconnected full domain receives a separate persistent map identity, while the existing parent plate retains control of its Euler motion and forcing. The recorded `domain` raster and `domains` metadata therefore distinguish visible connected geography from the `plate` raster's kinematic ownership. They do not add an opening impulse, a new physical boundary, or a separate stress response merely because raster support became disconnected.

This is a representational compromise: geographically disconnected domains can remain in one kinematic group until the existing stress-driven breakup or accretion rules change that membership. Their separate names improve history review but do not solve the mechanics that would determine whether detached lithosphere should move independently. Grid sampling can also lose real subgrid connections or briefly divide a narrow domain. Tiny empty ocean remnants can be absorbed locally across a shared boundary; preserving a buoyant material inventory does not authorize moving a remote continent to an unrelated neighboring plate.

Domain continuity uses inverse-advected, area-weighted raster overlap, matched only within the same persistent motion-plate UID. One previous identity can continue into one component: after a split, the strongest surviving overlap retains it and other components receive new UIDs linked by `source_domain_uid`. After a reunion, the strongest overlapping identity survives and the others retire. A domain that disappears and later reappears receives a new identity. The first domain of a motion plate uses its plate name; additional ones use **Fragment** names. New nonprimary domains produce `surface_fragment` events; reunions and retirements are represented by the frames. This is sampled geographical continuity, distinct from the enduring identity of a tracked material marker.

Inherited differences in thermal structure, composition, and rheology can influence rift segmentation and the course of breakup. That supports guiding synthetic fractures through weaker belts. It does not imply that real cratons are indestructible or that natural rifts follow this program's synthetic curves. Strong protection of connected mapped cratons is a selected worldbuilding rule, and conservative bounding regions can reject cuts that a richer mechanical model might permit. [Gouiza and Naliboff, 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-24945-5)

All initial ocean cells are assigned to one moving plate, recorded as **Primordial ocean**. Protection from loading-triggered rupture checks its persistent UID, so a later plate reusing the same storage slot does not inherit that protection. Disconnected ocean remnants receive their own domain identities while retaining that parent's motion. An arbitrary starting map with disconnected ocean basins can therefore have multiple visible domains belonging to one moving plate. The original ocean may shrink through boundary interactions and eventually disappear; complete consumption is not imposed at a scheduled date. A connected surrounding ocean begins without the old ocean-plate mosaic's internal boundaries. Island-arc development must follow the boundaries the new scenario actually creates.

Later breakup is controlled by a **kinematic internal-loading proxy**, not a stress tensor in pascals. The code measures relative boundary motion, forms weighted tensile, compressive, and shear contributions, fits the best rigid Euler response, and retains the residual that such a response cannot accommodate. Opposing boundary loads can create that residual even when their net torque is small. Plate-driving forces and crustal structure can affect stresses inside plates; the numerical proxy and all its scaling coefficients remain this model's approximation. [Zoback and Zoback, 2007](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/lithosphere-stress-and-deformation)

For new continental breakups, a persistent spherical material mesh now distributes local boundary-derived loading. Relative resistance depends on thickness, cratons, sutures, thermal support and damage; inherited rift directions influence reopening. Positive local extension progressively damages links and thins columns. A new plate requires a currently opening, sufficiently extended cut separating substantial connected material components while protecting whole patches and cratons. Compression cannot charge an opening timer. The former 110 Myr cooldown and 75 proxy×Myr continental threshold no longer authorize automatic continental rupture. [RIFTING.md](RIFTING.md) gives the implemented coefficients, memory, history fields and limits. The 180 Myr load-decay / 140 Myr cooldown / 95 proxy×Myr rule remains only for eligible ocean-only plates carrying juvenile buoyant material; the original ocean UID remains protected.

**The mantle-driving formulas and coefficients are unchanged.** Seeded initial background motion, its slow perturbation and decay, and boundary-traction feedback remain. Progressive continental breakthroughs remove the former fixed opening kick and added mantle bias: daughter responses are fitted to existing local loading, and both inherit the parent's mantle target. Initial partitioning, back-arc rules and the separate legacy ocean-fracture path retain their own motion rules. Changed geometry, loading and breakup timing can change the resulting trajectory even when the force calculation itself is unchanged.

Arc construction adds reference area at qualifying oceanic subduction fronts. Its precise conditions, growth rule, and visibility limits are described below.

For a progressive continental breakup, connected material components determine the developed cut. Its continuation through attached ocean is compared with shortest spherical grid paths from the resulting continental shores, restricted to the parent plate. The alternative is accepted only if it shortens the added ocean interface and passes the disconnected-area bound; otherwise the original continuation remains. This avoids extending a continental crack gratuitously along remote ocean strips. It does not forbid parallel ridges: ridge relocation and propagating spreading segments occur in nature, often with abandonment of an older axis. The length comparison is a geometric worldbuilding heuristic, not a fracture-energy calculation. [Mittelstaedt et al., 2011](https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/earthsciences_archive/FACULTY/ITO/Mittelstaedt_RepeatRidgeJumps_JGR11.pdf)

Boundary type comes from relative velocity at neighboring cells: the normal component distinguishes extension from convergence, and the tangential component identifies transform-dominated motion. Continental contact changes the classification to rift or collision. This classifies a coarse, already existing boundary network; it does not resolve the mechanical nucleation of a fault or prove that a new slab can penetrate the mantle. Subduction polarity is retained by plate pair where possible, with buoyancy-based overrides at continental margins.

Boundary normals now come from a local spherical fit to the two plates' fractional coverage, with subcell interface positions. Projected lengths reduce the excess length of an orthogonal raster staircase. Classification, boundary tractions, loading, trench relief and volcanic offsets all use that geometry. Ambiguous junctions and weak fits keep their original local geometry. This improves mechanical directions without forcing every subduction zone into an arc: reconstructed Mariana geometry, for example, includes a change from nearly straight to curved. [Weber et al., 2024](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2023GC011313)

A new **parameterized hinge retreat** extends overriding oceanic lithosphere toward the downgoing side, allowing a trench to migrate away from a coastline. The requested speed relative to the overriding plate is `min(8, 0.15 × convergence × clip(sqrt(ocean_age/80), 0.25, 1.2) / (1 + 2.5 × overriding_craton_fraction))` km/Myr. These coefficients are assumptions for worldbuilding. Real upper-plate extension and retreat depend on slab, thermal and rheological structure that this model does not solve. [Zhang and Davies, 2024](https://se.copernicus.org/articles/15/1113/2024/)

Retreat shifts fractional ocean support using inverse spherical sampling, with a 75 km directional stencil and explicit protection of buoyant footprints and ambiguous third-plate junctions. The displaced sample is compared with the same continuous reconstruction at zero displacement. Comparing it with the raw cell value manufactured finite transfer as the timestep approached zero in the native mesh. Residual downgoing support behind the front is included; no continental parcel is reassigned or created. `trench_swept_km2` records actual transferred membership separately from the existing ocean-consumption diagnostic. That transfer supplies a bounded extra arc-flux allowance only on nearby moving subduction segments. Each segment is limited to its local requested retreat rate, so a shortening boundary cannot concentrate the whole pair's transfer into extreme volcanic forcing. `trench_arc_sweep_km2` records the portion represented by this allowance; unallocated motion is not turned into extra magma. The allocation and semi-Lagrangian remap remain approximate and resolution dependent. There is no slab mesh. The support field carries forearc geometry, while separate local trench records now retain identities, polarity and lifecycle. Retreat events report the first transfer in each plate-pair/100 Myr bin; boundary maps retain the intervening geometry.

New topography combines an ocean age-depth curve and trench relief with compensated material columns, volcanic construction, rift cooling and foreland deflection. Starting basement relief is a smooth seeded spherical field sampled once into material columns and carried with them. Independent parcel white noise and the former fixed-coordinate snapshot roughness no longer create small sea-level crossings in new histories; fine procedural detail belongs to the later terrain builder. Buoyant columns start at neutral thicknesses of 35 km for mobile continents, 42 km for cratons and 25 km for juvenile arcs, anchored to their supplied full heights. Thickness is bounded to 8–75 km. Positive exposed height drives a simplified gross-denudation request; actual rock removal thins the column and rebounds through the same Airy balance. Net erosion lowering equals gross denudation minus rebound. The prior signed relief-decay rule and parcel-overlap elevation bonus remain properties of older saved engines, not the current column model.

Actual extension creates bounded thermal support attached to the thinned material. Support decays over 63 Myr after rifting, leaving a persistent thinned margin that can continue to subside. A separate same-owner foreland proxy follows current finite collision segments: zero within a 60 km peak belt, deepest around 250 km, ending by 440 km and capped at 1,800 m. It loads over 12 Myr and relaxes over 100 Myr. These worldbuilding coefficients approximate accommodation; they do not solve temperature fields, elastic flexure or sediment infill. Initial structural rifts carry no invented heat or past extension. [FOUNDATIONS.md](FOUNDATIONS.md) gives the equations, scientific sources and numerical limits.

Local trench systems now retain polarity, geometry, observed shortening and initiation, maturity, quieting, shutdown and reactivation records. Maturity depends on both active duration and accumulated convergence, using 10 Myr and 100 km scales. Current convergent oceanic contacts support arc supply in proportion to maturity. Brief inactivity preserves memory; longer buoyant blockage, extension, disappearance or owner loss ends an episode. Trench separation and local host transfer inherit appropriate history rather than being counted as wholly new mature systems. This is a parameterized lifecycle, not solved slab depth or physical proof that initiation succeeded. Local terrane accretion and subduction histories are detailed in [FOUNDATIONS.md](FOUNDATIONS.md).

The implemented GDH1-style background ocean depth uses `2600 + 365*sqrt(age)` metres for ages below 20 Myr, then `5651 - 2473*exp(-0.0278*age)` metres. The old-ocean depth tends toward 5,651 m rather than sinking indefinitely. The two published-fit branches have a small joining offset; this is negligible at the model's tectonic resolution. This curve supplies thermal bathymetry only, and does not supply missing slab dynamics or make ancient surviving seafloor geologically well constrained. [Stein and Stein, 1992](https://www.nature.com/articles/359123a0)

The simulation grid defaults to 512 × 256 and accepts dimensions through 2,048 × 1,024. This changes the fields actually advected, classified, and deformed at each step. Coordinate calculations, rotations, and finite-footprint deposition use bounded chunks to limit temporary memory. The new deposition and fracture-search work changes performance, so timings from the earlier point-deposition engine are historical evidence rather than estimates for this revision.

At the equator, nominal spacing of a global equirectangular width `W` is approximately `40,030 / W` km: 78.2 km at width 512, 39.1 km at 1,024, and 19.5 km at 2,048. Longitude cells narrow toward the poles. Small islands, individual mountain valleys, and narrow forearcs below the simulation spacing remain unresolved. Boundary symbols map the resolved interaction zone, not surveyed faults. Some deformation rules distribute uplift over neighboring cells, so their effective belt width changes with resolution. A larger grid is not evidence of resolution-independent geology.

### How island arcs form, and what this model actually does

In nature, an oceanic plate descends beneath another plate at a subduction zone. Water and other volatiles released from the descending plate promote melting in the mantle above it. Magma rises through the overriding plate; where that plate is oceanic, volcanoes can build an island arc, while a continental overriding plate carries a continental volcanic arc. The arc lies behind the trench on the overriding side. This source of magma is distinct from prescribing mantle-driven plate motion. [USGS: subduction zone](https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/subduction-zone-3), [USGS: introduction to subduction zones](https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/subduction-zone-science/science/introduction-subduction-zones-amazing-events)

The implementation uses the following explicit rules:

1. **A converging boundary must qualify as subduction.** Relative normal speed must be more negative than `-max(2, 0.35 × tangential_speed)` in km/Myr, and at least one adjacent cell must be oceanic. Two continental cells collide instead. Established local trench polarity persists while the incoming side is oceanic; new local contacts choose older oceanic crust where appropriate, with ocean-under-continent buoyancy overrides. A newly initiating system begins with zero arc-supply maturity.
2. **Place the volcanic front on the overriding side.** The target is **180 km from the reconstructed trench interface**, using an exact spherical offset along its local normal. This replaces the old offset from the adjacent cell centre, which added an unwanted resolution-dependent distance. If the target falls on another plate it still falls back to the adjacent overriding cell. The fixed distance is an approximation, not a calculation from slab angle or melting depth; real trench-to-arc distances vary. [Stern, 2002](https://personal.utdallas.edu/~rjstern/pdfs/SubductionZonesRoG.pdf)
3. **Build and replenish juvenile arcs.** Targets mapped as ocean or existing juvenile arc receive `(convergence + realized_retreat + 8) × boundary_length × timestep × 0.015 × maturity` of buoyant reference area, using km/Myr, km and Myr. Continental and cratonic targets receive column construction but no added juvenile area. At full maturity and with no retreat, convergence of 50 km/Myr along 1,000 km of trench for 2 Myr adds **1,740 km²**. These are heuristic reference-area additions, not erupted magma volume or immediate emerged-island area.
4. **Accumulate material before it becomes visible, then continue growing it.** Additions persist as moving juvenile parcels with an initial internal relief of 850 m. Rasterized crust appears at 36% cell-area density; the arc category requires juvenile material to exceed 55% of buoyant material. Emergence remains resolution dependent. The former gate stopped magma addition once an arc became visible; the corrected gate continues supplying existing juvenile arcs. This is not an explicit volcano growing from the sea floor to sea level. Subthreshold arc relief is still absent from the ocean elevation raster, and enlarging the final heightmap cannot recover unrecorded small islands.
5. **Carry and eventually accrete the arc.** Arc parcels move with their overriding plate. Sustained local collision can transfer a connected terrane to its receiver while remote source material remains on its old plate. Accreted juvenile material becomes continental in the map classification while retaining its column and origin history. The internal relief datum is adjusted so classification alone does not raise the surface. An old accreted arc can remain in the terrain and history after its orange arc colour disappears.

The front also receives a column-construction request proportional to convergence plus realized retreat (`1.25 × effective convergence × maturity` in the model's metres-per-Myr scaling). Strength, height saturation and available column thickness bound realized elevation gain. Continental targets receive this construction but no separate volcanic lithology. Arc erosion removes rock and includes rebound; unresolved additions can replenish an existing arc toward a relief floor through actual column addition, recorded as an explicit marker adjustment. Same-owner, same-cell juvenile parcels are periodically combined while preserving reference area, weighted full height and effective column volume.

To review an arc, show **Crust type** and the subduction boundaries, then follow its orange juvenile crust through successive epochs and inspect its material history. An `island_arc` catalogue entry reports the first addition recorded for a parent plate in each 100 Myr reporting bin. Its area is the addition in that event's integration step, not the total for the bin and not a count of individual volcanoes. Continuous boundaries and the saved maps provide the finer time history between these catalogue entries.

An audit of all 501 saved frames in the historical 192 × 96 run `20260905-214959-7ccc94` found 7.30 million km² of generated juvenile reference crust but only 32 arc-classified cells at 1,000 Myr, 30 of them north of 80°N; these cell counts are not island counts. Its older engine's plate-wide welding at 920 Myr reclassified about 629,344 km² of mapped arc area when Rift plate 04 joined Rift plate 06. That run predates the column and local-accretion revisions and remains unchanged. It demonstrates how coarse visibility and reclassification can hide an arc's history; sparse arc-origin markers cannot establish missing island footprints or justify adding emerged land to a higher-resolution export.

Starting with one ocean plate means there are initially no internal ocean–ocean plate boundaries. An island arc therefore needs later plate geometry to place oceanic overriding crust beside a converging oceanic boundary; subduction beneath the starting continent alone does not guarantee an offshore island chain. The model now records initiation and maturity but still does not solve physical subduction initiation. Arc formation is not directly switched off by setting slab pull to zero if other retained forces produce qualifying convergence and sustained trench maturity.

There is no explicit slab dip/depth, water or melt budget, magma plumbing, dynamically solved rollback, or individual volcanic cone calculation. The new maturation delay, hinge retreat and back-arc rules are parameterizations. They give terrain a reviewable tectonic origin without predicting a real island arc's detailed geology or emergence chronology. The mantle-motion formulas remain unchanged.

### When a spreading rift is subducted

A spreading ridge entering a trench can open a slab window and alter the overriding plate's heat flow, volcanism, deformation and topography. Modeling of the Chile Triple Junction supports forearc heating, broad or displaced magmatism and later uplift, while the observed outcomes depend on the tectonic setting. This does not imply that every encounter creates a giant mountain chain. [Sanhueza et al., 2023](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2023GC010977)

The implementation requires an **active oceanic spreading boundary meeting a subduction boundary through a common downgoing cell**, three distinct plate UIDs, incoming ridge crust no older than 20 Myr, ridge–trench separation at most 350 km, and estimated ridge-axis motion toward the overriding plate faster than 0.5 km/Myr. Ridge-axis motion is approximated by the mean Euler motion of its two plates. Young seafloor alone, a continental rift, a receding ridge or a distant backarc ridge does not qualify. The adjacency and physical-distance tests can miss an encounter below the chosen grid's resolution.

An encounter starts a **32 Myr episode**, peaking after **8 Myr**, approximately 180 km into the overriding plate. Its spherical footprint has a **450 km radius**, with a smooth taper to zero at its edge. It adds up to **400 m of temporary thermal support** at the center; overlapping support is capped at 600 m. That component rises and fades with the episode instead of accumulating each step. A separate lifetime request of up to **200 m of volcanic construction** is integrated over each interval, reduced by crustal strength, height saturation and available column thickness, then affected by ordinary erosion. In new column histories this adds physical column thickness at fixed effective area; it does not create buoyant reference area or guarantee new islands. These distances, amplitudes and times are worldbuilding parameters, not universal measurements of slab windows.

The footprint moves with its overriding plate and follows physical host transfers. Contacts within 600 km of an existing episode on that host refresh a latch without restarting the pulse or replenishing its construction budget. Latches persist for 80 Myr after the last encounter. This suppresses numerical retriggering and can also combine genuinely distinct nearby encounters; sufficiently far migration can leave a sequence of separate footprints. Stable episode and plate identities survive checkpoints and plate-slot reuse.

History records **ridge subduction**, **slab-window peak**, and **slab-window cooling**. New frames carry active `ridge_episodes` for the dotted map overlay. `trace_ridge_uplift_m` is the cumulative volcanic contribution **already included in** `trace_uplift_m`; it must not be added twice. `trace_ridge_thermal_m` is the current temporary support, outside the internal relief ledger. Saved elevations include both effects, so final terrain and geometric goSPL forcing inherit them. For new column histories, goSPL uses recorded net erosion, which already excludes this temporary overlay. Its legacy analytical fallback subtracts reconstructed temporary support before estimating old relief decay, preserving cooling as subsidence rather than fictitious erosion recovery.

Ordinary arc production and the mantle-driving forces retain their existing rules. This surface-effects closure does not solve slab tearing, a three-dimensional window, melt chemistry, an ordinary-arc shutdown, or emplacement of an ophiolite. Original crustal weights and coherent material patches are retained. It is separate from an old continental rift being compressed closed.

### Back-arc basin formation

Back-arc extension occurs within an overriding plate, behind a volcanic arc. Retreat relative to the plate interior can promote extension, but subduction alone does not guarantee a basin. Arc-related weakness and overriding-lithosphere strength also matter. This implementation follows that qualitative relationship without imposing a universal slab-age cutoff or adding a mantle circulation model. [Sdrolias and Müller, 2006](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2005GC001090); [Zhang and Davies, 2024](https://se.copernicus.org/articles/15/1113/2024/index.html).

The existing forearc transfer supplies a realized retreat rate relative to the overriding plate, limited by convergence, ocean age and cratonic resistance. Current trench maturity scales its back-arc loading contribution. The strongest local sector of each plate pair is considered within **2,200 km**. Actual oceanic subduction, at least **800 km** of contact and mean retreat above **0.4 km/Myr** are required. Loading has an **80 Myr** relaxation time and **60 km** rupture threshold, scaled by the existing rift-strength control. Because demand relaxes toward retreat rate times rift strength times that memory instead of accumulating without bound, the pair fixes the sustained retreat a basin needs to rupture at all: **0.75 km/Myr** at a rift strength of 1. Run `20260917-0012-nonpenetration` sustained about 0.55 km/Myr, so none of its 20 back-arc basins opened in 152 Myr; the two most loaded reached 45.6 and 42.3 km. A local 150 Myr/45 km alternative is held separately (see [RUPTURE-CALIBRATION.md](RUPTURE-CALIBRATION.md)). It lowers the strength-1 equilibrium crossover to 0.3 km/Myr, below the independent 0.4 km/Myr admission gate, so admission would then bind instead; it is not calibrated either. It represents kinematic demand, not basin width or stress in pascals. Under this swept-retreat driver, time, plate size, common rigid rotation and stale subduction labels cannot generate it.

**Moving-hinge worlds (back-arc driver 1).** A fresh reviewed world with `moving_hinge_v1` has no forearc transfer, so it has no swept retreat. There the loading rate is the overriding plate's own trench-normal velocity away from its trench, in the stationary-mantle frame the force balance uses. It counts only on attached subduction edges whose retained slab reaches below the overriding lithosphere (slab length x sin 50 deg greater than the cooling-law thickness, capped at 100 km). That depth condition is the driver's own: the balance applies its anchor, scaled by slab length, to every attached slab. The speed is the hinge lag the balance's slab anchor resists: the sea anchor of Uyeda and Kanamori (1979). Because it is measured in the stationary-mantle frame, a common (net) rotation that moves an upper plate away from its anchored slab does load it. That is deliberate: the balance resists net rotation through drag, and Scotese's Rule VII ties margin style to absolute plate motion. In SEP21T net rotation supplies up to about a quarter of the one loading intra-oceanic margin's speed. Heuret and Lallemand (2005) observed that back-arc extension follows an upper plate moving away from its trench, and compression follows one advancing. The sector, length, admission, memory and rupture rules above are unchanged. After a rupture the arc's opening comes from the force re-solve. Saved worlds keep the swept-retreat driver. Upper plates that advance on their trenches, which is most of them in slow worlds, never load. This driver is kinematic. The opt-in force-limit rifting of PR #189 (not yet merged) would test the same margins in stress: its loads include the slab anchor drag. The two keep separate ledgers and never feed each other, and a pending back-arc rupture commits before a force-limit tear in the same step. With both enabled, a margin ruptures on whichever criterion is met first. See [PHYSICS_REPAIRS.md](PHYSICS_REPAIRS.md), "Back-arc loading under the moving hinge".

After at least **8 Myr** of loading, a dedicated cut may detach an arc strip. Its gently varying inland edge lies nominally **550 km** behind the trench (bounded to **350–900 km**), behind the model's **180 km** volcanic front. The candidate stays within a **2,500 km** sector. Child and surviving parent must stay connected; the child must contain arc-margin material, touch its parent and downgoing plate, avoid third plates and preserve complete material patches and enduring craton groups. Cratonic footprints near the new rupture reject it. Obstructed or unresolved candidates remain intact and can be retried. This narrow-margin rule does not require the balanced halves used for generic continental breakup.

The arc inherits the parent's mantle vector. Initial differential Euler motion comes from measured retreat, with area-weighted parent recoil. Subsequently a transient correction approaches locally supported retreat with a **12 Myr** response time, using current relative velocities. It does not overwrite strong compression or accelerate already faster extension. The ordinary mantle-force method and coefficients are unchanged. The separated arc's trench no longer also receives forearc remapping, avoiding duplicate retreat. Generic fracture protection for active back-arc hosts expires after **60 Myr** without activity.

No basin or elevation trough is painted at rupture. Real plate movement, the existing ocean transport, extensional deformation and arc-growth rules evolve its geography. Continental stretching enters the existing rift memory and can later invert. Opening is measured from length-weighted positive normal motion at the parent–arc boundary; cumulative extension is not exact geographic width. Oceanic spreading requires **300 km** of oceanic divergent boundary and mean opening above **1 km/Myr**. Ten Myr without appreciable opening records quieting; extension can resume. Actual cumulative shortening can consume recorded opening, and incorporation can end the separate plate boundary. Age alone does not close a basin.

Saved records separate retreat loading from measured opening and retain UIDs, phases and milestone events. Terrain and goSPL use the ordinary saved elevations and material history, with no second artificial back-arc depression. Sector aggregation, fixed distances, rigid arc plates and simplified rheology are worldbuilding approximations. Coarse grids may miss narrow basins or reject unresolved splits. The model does not resolve slab dip, thermal weakening, melt supply or three-dimensional mantle flow.

### Inversion of an inherited continental rift

Previously extended continental basins can concentrate later shortening and become part of mountain belts. Inheritance, fault orientation and rock strength influence the resulting geometry; inversion does not always mean simply reversing the same normal faults. Analogue experiments and field observations show several styles of inversion, including oblique and along-basin shortening. [Sieberer et al., 2023](https://se.copernicus.org/articles/14/647/2023/)

Continental material now retains an explicit rift identity, creation time, tangent along the curved rift, cumulative observed extensional depression, and realized inversion uplift. Initial accepted fractures seed structural identity only: their reservoir starts at zero, so the initial map is unchanged and there is no invented prehistory of stretching. Later continental breakup requests 250 m of lowering through column thinning and records only the realized loss, bounded by the minimum thickness. Actual mechanical extensional lowering adds to the reservoir; thermal cooling and foreland subsidence do not. At intersecting rifts, each material sample retains its earliest recorded scar and direction. This is one inheritance direction per sample, not a full fault network. Collision sutures and juvenile arc production do not create continental rift memory.

Current convergent boundary motion supplies a bounded compression proxy. Continental collision can load both plates; subduction can load the overriding plate. The actual relative Euler velocity must contain appreciable convergence, so a stale boundary label, common rigid motion, shear alone, or scar age cannot trigger uplift. Compression is transmitted only from boundaries touching the sample's current owner and at most **1,800 km** away, with a smooth distance taper and a **300 km** contact-length scale. A parallel-transported compression direction is compared with the material's cross-rift direction. The squared alignment is a simple cross-rift preference; it does not reproduce all natural oblique or along-strike inversion mechanisms. This is a spatially limited kinematic approximation, not a solved stress field or connectivity-aware rheological model.

The lifetime inversion capacity at a material sample is **three times its recorded extensional depression, capped at 2,000 m**. An exponential approach to that capacity limits the initial unsaturated rate to **30 m/Myr**, with weaker or more distant compression giving less. Craton resistance, relief saturation and ordinary erosion still apply. Active local extension prevents simultaneous inversion. Further actual stretching can increase available capacity up to the same cap; elapsed time and repeated detections cannot refill it. The request now thickens a material column with reciprocal effective-area adjustment; only realized compensated uplift consumes the inversion reservoir, and the 75 km thickness cap can stop further gain. These worldbuilding rules do not move or delete coherent crust patches, alter buoyant reference area, or change the mantle-force equations.

Scar tangents rotate with their host material across poles and the longitude seam. Welding and later splits retain inherited identity and consumed capacity. Events record inversion onset or reactivation at appreciable rates (at least 0.1 m/Myr in an affected sample), and a quiet event after 10 Myr below that reporting threshold. A quiet event is a reporting state, not proof that every infinitesimal deformation rate is zero. Regional history separates structural origin, first saved stretching evidence and first saved inversion evidence. The optional `trace_rift_extension_m` is already included in total extension loss, and `trace_inversion_uplift_m` is already included in total uplift. The elevation field and normal erosion counter carry the effect into final terrain and goSPL, with no extra uplift added during export.

### What a reviewable material history means

For worldbuilding, the history is a product in its own right: it should expose how a mountain belt, accreted island arc, or abandoned rift emerged in the simulated world. New runs save a frame every 2 Myr by default and record passive representative markers in buoyant crust. The new standard and larger grids sample up to 16,384 starting parcels and 4,096 later arc additions; smaller grids use lower caps recorded in frame metadata. The markers move with their host plates' Euler rotations and follow the engine's splitting and local-accretion rules. Their identities survive later arc-parcel coalescence. They are a bounded sample of material trajectories, not a complete inventory of uniquely identified rock. Raising these limits does not add missing markers to previously saved runs.

The inspector distinguishes a **material history**, which follows one representative marker, from a **location history**, which samples a fixed geographic coordinate as different material moves through it. Association requires a nearby marker on the selected cell's plate and reports the selection-to-marker distance. Oceanic material has no persistent marker history in this implementation. Legacy runs lack material markers and retain fixed-location inspection; missing trajectories are not reconstructed from changing plate colors. A later-born arc begins at its recorded birth time, while a marker present at time zero has no inferred age or pre-simulation origin history.

The history separates a marker's internal relief from the mapped surface elevation sampled at its moving position. For `structure_version: 1`, the ledger is birth relief plus uplift minus tectonic lowering minus net erosion plus adjustments. The existing `trace_extension_m` field now includes mechanical extension, rift cooling and increasing foreland deflection. `trace_erosion_m` is actual net lowering after denudation rebound; gross denudation and rebound are diagnostic components, not additional uplift or erosion terms. Replenishment and class-datum corrections are explicit adjustments. The mechanical rift and inversion counters remain subsets of their corresponding totals. Older saved histories preserve their original extensional-loss, signed-relaxation and clipping conventions; negative legacy erosion could mean negative relief relaxed upward.

Mapped elevation uses deposited full column heights, ocean thermal bathymetry and the temporary slab-window overlay. The initial basement relief is already included in the column heights. A marker is one representative column, so its counters cannot reconstruct an arbitrary raster pixel or a mixture of overlapping material. During overlap, the transported surface owner remains exposed while its own current finite footprint independently meets the crust visibility threshold; otherwise the dominant arriving deposit takes over. This avoids treating sampling differences between overlapping sheets as geological boundaries and does not move hidden material between plates. Optional thickness, thermal support, cooling age, foreland deflection and gross/net erosion fields explain the new history without claiming calibrated rock uplift or sediment flux. Their units and interpretation are in [FOUNDATIONS.md](FOUNDATIONS.md).

Plate UIDs identify plate generations even when raster plate IDs are reused. Structured events link involved plate UIDs with a time, type, description, and an approximate location when available. The whole-run catalogue remains accessible while viewing an earlier snapshot. Related events are selected by the marker's host plate at the event epoch, so an event may concern a remote segment of that plate. They are context for interpreting the local trajectory, not evidence that every listed event occurred at the marker. Saved boundary rasters supply the resolved local interaction history; the event catalogue does not enumerate every ongoing subduction segment at every step.

New per-plate `internal_loading` records expose the current dimensionless load proxy, boundary-speed terms in km/Myr, accumulated proxy×Myr, the rupture threshold, cooldown, eligibility, and original-ocean protection. Fracture events retain the triggering loading diagnostics and plate UIDs. A threshold ratio indicates the state of this particular algorithm; it is neither a probability of fracture nor measured stress. Initial and legacy frames can lack the diagnostic entirely. Material records also expose `trace_patch` and `trace_craton` where recorded. Original patch IDs persist; juvenile arc patch IDs can change during coalescence, while marker `trace_id` remains the trajectory key. Regional history distinguishes a recorded visible domain name and UID from the actual motion plate's identity. Existing histories cannot be retroactively given new fracture paths, corrected crust geometry, or missing loading and material-group records.

The intended downstream sequence is tectonic framework, landscape development, and climate modelling. The separate goSPL adapter now converts a selected saved range into a native closed spherical mesh and timed horizontal and vertical forcing. Mesh coordinates and elevations use metres; rates use metres/year; elapsed source Myr are rebased to years since the selected first epoch. `advect: interp` receives finite chord rates connecting original and rotated vertex coordinates, matching goSPL's endpoint displacement and remapping method. Material marker fits recover plate rotations where possible, with recorded Euler approximations for untracked ocean plates and reported residuals for intervals spanning non-rigid motion. [GoSPL tectonic forcing specification](https://gospl.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user_guide/optfile2.html)

Vertical forcing is geometric surface change evaluated along that motion, plus a correction from matched material erosion counters on an owner/crust-restricted grid of at most 256 × 128. For column histories it adds back **net denudation after rebound**, removing the program's simplified erosion and its associated rebound together. Unsupported nodes use the saved last-step net erosion rate as an approximate interval rate, with support coverage reported. Legacy histories keep their signed-relaxation correction and bounded analytical fallback. Rift cooling and foreland deflection already affect saved height; they are not added again as separate forcing. The package retains separate geometric and correction arrays and source reference epochs. Coastal sampling, overlapping material, ownership changes, sparse markers and goSPL remapping prevent exact epoch reproduction. This is not a complete physical uplift budget. See [GOSPL.md](GOSPL.md) and the [official geometric forcing example](https://github.com/Geodels/goSPL-examples/blob/main/shared_scripts/umeshFcts.py).

### Derived terrain and what its history means

The separate terrain builder creates a surface for a selected saved epoch. Its default output is 8,192 × 4,096; production options range from 2,048 × 1,024 to 16,384 × 8,192. The default has 4.9 km pixels at the equator, while the largest option has 2.4 km pixels. These are sampling distances for synthesized topography. They do not add tectonic cells, active faults, or material trajectories to the source history.

The builder interpolates source relief and conditions additional ridged and multiscale spherical noise using relief gradients, cratons, arcs, collision and subduction proximity, rifts, ocean age, and available marker suture and cumulative-uplift envelopes. Cratonic interiors receive less ruggedness. A bounded displacement of source coordinates varies coastlines and interior features by a fraction of a source cell. A smoothed coarse residual is removed to limit introduced broad elevation bias. This is a procedural worldbuilding construction; it does not conserve eroded sediment or solve drainage, hillslope transport, flexure, or a new physical uplift budget.

The noise uses the same seed and **fixed world coordinates** at every epoch. Geological conditioning follows each epoch's recorded fields and moving markers, but individual added ridges and shoreline details do not move with crustal material. Regenerating an unchanged source epoch with the same settings is deterministic; comparing different epochs does not yield a tracked history for each fine terrain pixel. Fine topographic differences therefore cannot be interpreted as rock uplift, erosion, or horizontal displacement.

Terrain provenance preserves the exact selected source frame, source resolution, run identity, elapsed time, configuration, available saved engine source and local dependencies, and terrain builder fingerprint. `engine_sha256` identifies the main engine only; `auxiliary_sources_sha256` records the captured local import closure, including the geometry, material, column, accretion and trench helpers used by that saved engine. Terrain metadata carries the corresponding source-helper fingerprints. Export checks the saved bytes rather than substituting current source into an earlier world. Legacy histories without helper records keep their original provenance. The complete tectonic history remains a separate export. The detailed float32 NPY stores metres relative to model sea level; its 16-bit PNG rounds to the nearest metre with `elevation_m = pixel_value - 12000`. That numerical encoding precision is unrelated to geological accuracy.

These derived surfaces can serve as base topography for subsequent landscape preparation, image rendering, and climate boundary-condition development. The evolving goSPL adapter uses recorded tectonic grids and material counters, rather than the fixed-coordinate procedural detail, for its initial mesh and interval forcing. A ready-to-run ROCKE-3D configuration still requires separate climate boundary-condition preparation.

## Corrections and limits to the supplied rules of thumb

The [preserved source](RULES-OF-THUMB.md) retains its original wording. Its 10 cm/year conversion is approximate and overstates the equivalent distance by about 11 percent; the exact conversion above is used for interpretation.

Its final-page sea-level argument associates decreasing mean ocean-lithosphere age with increased ocean-basin capacity. The thermal-age contribution has the opposite sign: **younger ocean lithosphere is generally hotter and shallower**, while older lithosphere is deeper. Removing a spreading ridge and preventing new young crust may subsequently increase mean age, but that is a different causal statement. Sea level additionally depends on water volume and the entire basin geometry. [Stein and Stein, 1992](https://www.nature.com/articles/359123a0)

Statements about an entirely passive mantle, an exact slab-pull fraction, mandatory ridge/trench alignments, an immutable maximum plate speed, collision as the sole possible end of subduction, or universal arc locations are not treated as hard physical laws. A rule of thumb may summarize a common configuration while failing for another geometry, rheology, or reference frame. The model's numerical speed limits, split timing, polarity selection, and event thresholds are documented implementation choices; matching such limits does not constitute scientific validation.

### Computational choice: continental rupture strain

The [mode-specific rupture calibration](RUPTURE-CALIBRATION.md) uses a 0.15
accumulated legacy loading proxy and retains the enhanced mode's 0.35 accumulated
realized logarithmic strain. The legacy quarter partition is not present in the
enhanced measure. These thresholds are numerical closures selected for different
mechanical representations, not a universal physical rupture strain. Current
opening, damage, coherent material cuts, protected cratons and resolved daughter
geometry remain necessary. Scotese's Rule III motivates extension-driven breakup
but provides no such threshold; this is a documented computational simplification.
The local coarse-workload scan motivates the legacy selection without establishing
resolution convergence or geological calibration. Historical strain, source and
run records are preserved, and no live run migration follows from this change.

### Computational correction: persistent force-rift material paths

The [block 04 force-rift repair](FORCE-RIFT-MATERIAL-PATH.md) separates a
developing tear's material identity from its changing control-cell projection.
The selected tear must move with its plate and retain represented material
ancestry, while current forces and strengths determine each newly paid opening
increment. A genuinely replaced cut starts a new path; historical virtual
speeds cannot supply missing work. This is consistent with Scotese's load-driven
rifting and inherited-weakness principles, not an additional plate-driving
force. The retained rigid-until-breakup approximation still does not resolve
gradual material necking. The linked note distinguishes implementation,
validation and live-run adoption.

### Computational choice: bounded projected ownership cleanup

After a material fracture, [bounded projection cleanup](PROJECTION-CLEANUP.md)
can remove small control-label fragments only when the original daughter map
already passes the existing 2.5% per-side and resolved-interior checks. It locks
cells containing parent material and rechecks the cleaned proposal. A large lobe
cannot be assigned to the other plate to make a disconnected split pass. This
is a numerical representation choice; Scotese's Rule III still motivates the
required extension-driven material fracture and supplies no fragment threshold.
The distinction between material identity and plate ownership remains explicit.
Reported reassigned area describes cleanup before any separate raster coastal
continuation; it is not an inventory of every final plate-ownership change.

### Computational clarification: native rift attachment

The [native rift contact correction](NATIVE-RIFT-CONTACT.md) prevents an opening
ownership contour from imposing tensile loading on already separated material
under native material mechanics version 1. The earlier indexed-bridge check
could reattach just one shore when the reconstructed contour crossed its
triangles; this was measured at the saved 7–9 boundary. Native owner cuts have
separate vertices, so a contour crossing one body's interior does not establish
a connection to the other plate. Ocean production and actual convergent contact
retain their existing paths. This is a computational correction consistent
with Rules I and III, not a requirement for symmetric natural margins. It
removes the opening-contour surrogate for native continental extension; a
separate mechanism transmitting attached slab traction into internal material
tension remains unimplemented. The linked note distinguishes the tested repair
from that remaining physical limitation and from live-run adoption.

### Computational clarification: retained-phase column floor

The ordinary 8 km crustal-column floor is a numerical mechanics reserve, not a
claim that metamorphosed crust cannot occupy a thinner physical volume. Retained
phase conversion conserves mass while increasing density, so the exact volume
contraction of dense material still retained in the column is restored before
the reserve is tested. Mantle return removes mass and therefore consumes the
reserve; zero computed drainage cannot be replaced with an invented detachment
event. Return is limited to the reserve actually available: dense phase that
the reserve cannot release stays in the column as a load and is reported in
every frame, because explicit parcel retirement or oceanization of an
exhausted sliver is not implemented and dropping or rescaling mass would be
worse than retaining it. This same restored-thickness invariant bounds
deformation and erosion and sets the juvenile gravitational reference.
Physical thickness must remain positive. The derivation, the reserve-limited
drainage closure and remaining limits are in
[PHASE-ADJUSTED-COLUMN-FLOOR.md](PHASE-ADJUSTED-COLUMN-FLOOR.md).
The policy is saved separately as retained-phase floor version 2; historical
version-1 frames remain readable, while live evolution requires an explicit
policy migration.

This clarification concerns lower-crust bookkeeping rather than a plate-driving
rule in Scotese's guide. It introduces no mantle conveyor or imposed plate
motion, and therefore is not a departure from the adopted rules of thumb.

### Implementation departure: subduction persistence

Scotese's Rule IV emphasizes long-lived subduction and difficult initiation. The current implementation retains attached-slab pull through brief interruptions, and adds slab area and excess mass only from actual committed ocean consumption. It does not guarantee continuing intake: incoming ocean, actual convergence, and available finite overlap must all be present. Spreading produces new ocean at eligible divergent boundaries; there is no automatic replacement-rift or supply controller that keeps every trench fed.

The trench lifecycle nevertheless uses prescribed shutdown delays: 10 Myr of sustained extension, 20 Myr of quiet conditions or lost contact, and 6 Myr of buoyant collision; loss of a plate owner can terminate an episode immediately. Shutdown disables direct slab pull even when retained slab inventory is nonzero. Inventory also retires exponentially on a 50 Myr timescale. These are computational lifecycle proxies, not resolved slab detachment or proof that the slab's physical connection has failed. See [trench_history.py](trench_history.py) and [slab_memory.py](slab_memory.py).

This departure can extinguish the driving force after a prolonged stall without a large continental collision. It requires review before claiming dynamically self-sustaining subduction. A stronger future treatment would diagnose trench-relative ocean supply, the force budget, and continued slab attachment, with an explicit reason for termination rather than a minimum imposed intake. Real termination without continental collision is possible, including loss of slab attachment during ridge approach; that physical exception does not validate the current timers. [Burkett and Billen, 2009](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2009JB006402)

The opt-in `slab_tether_native.advance` experiment now selects rupture-governed trench shutdown. While a local neck retains attached slab mass on an existing two-plate trace, a quiet, extending, or buoyant-collision label does not cut off its force after a fixed delay. When every local neck has actually ruptured and its inventory has been retired, the trench shuts down with that reason; a still-matched boundary color cannot immediately reactivate those detached channels. The historical timer remains the law for ordinary and restored older worlds. A lost plate owner or vanished physical trace can still end the episode through the existing lifecycle, and a later replacement trench now requires a separate initiation or polarity-reversal law. The coupled path's local neck state is a reduced rupture closure, not a resolved three-dimensional slab tear.

The same coupled path now admits subduction capture and continued trench history only where a live historical trace has an attached, mass-bearing local neck with its original downgoing plate. A new convergent color, expired trace memory, or opposite polarity cannot create or mature a replacement slab. This is a conservative gate for the missing initiation and reversal physics, not a prediction that natural subduction cannot start. The explicit initial geological condition must therefore supply a slab inventory and neck law; independently initiated trenches require a future mechanical law before they can enter this path.

Automatic continental entry in the coupled path also requires that attached local slab. A detached remembered trace cannot register new incoming faces. If a registered entry region loses its source slab, force/source evaluation fails atomically: the model has no law yet for releasing hinge work, retaining deeply entered crust, or eduction/rebound after breakoff. This is a deliberate unresolved-physics boundary rather than a claim that continental material vanishes or stays fixed after detachment.

The isolated candidate channel now has a mathematically reciprocal contact and handoff force, but its local path spring is a computational surrogate, **not** resolved lithospheric bending. A true elastic flexure law couples neighboring deflections through curvature, with effective strength varying across and along real trenches; the candidate spring charges energy even for a uniform depth offset. The [flexure closure audit](CHANNEL_FLEXURE_AUDIT.md) derives this distinction and documents the still-missing upper basal reference, source histories and post-breakoff path. This is not a disagreement with Scotese's collision or suture rules: those rules do not determine a vertical constitutive law, and the native active-overlap guard remains in place. [Zhang et al., 2018](https://academic.oup.com/gji/article/215/3/2071/5105755)

The [channel source audit](CHANNEL_SOURCE_AUDIT.md) shows why the existing ambient-mantle entry pressure and overlying-crust stack pressure cannot both be applied over one active intersection: that would count the upper crust's vertical space twice. Its read-only layered overburden includes upper rock, surface water and only the mantle gap beneath the current upper base. The model still lacks a coupled contact-depth, thermal and phase-history transaction, so this accounting check does not lift the active-overlap guard.

## How to assess a generated world

Review several times, not just the final heightmap. Check that rifts accompany relative extension, convergence consumes oceanic material, arcs occur on the overriding side, buoyant material survives principally through collision or accretion, and mountains persist and decay after active boundaries migrate. A land outline is not proof of continental crust, and a boundary color is not proof that a complete physical subduction system was solved.

Compare multiple seeds and at least two resolutions before relying on a particular mountain belt or terrane trajectory. Persistent large-scale outcomes are more informative than a single detailed coastline. Saved numerical fields and event history support inspection and reproducibility of this program; they do not establish geological uniqueness.

The test suite checks repeatability, spherical geometry, finite fields, reference-area retention, live ownership of buoyant parcels, and accumulated motion smaller than one raster cell. Material-coherence checks additionally exercise whole-patch assignment, hidden craton protection, and internal spherical distances; domain checks distinguish disconnected continents from disconnected full plates. A fixed seed-12, 128 by 64 default-world regression also checks for continued resolved plate boundaries through 1,000 Myr and visible island-arc crust somewhere in the history. Its requirement for at least three plates after 200 Myr detects a previously stalled default model; it is not a law that every arbitrary initial condition must satisfy. Area retention and rigid-rotation tests alone previously passed despite later tearing; these are different properties and require separate checks.

Time step and resolution remain material sources of uncertainty. Bilinear transport diffuses narrow plates and age fronts; mapped coastlines, arc visibility, collision thresholds, and splits can change with cell size. Shared-field ridge reconstruction has a bounded refinement check, but that does not establish resolution-independent world evolution. Snapshot cadence can subdivide integration steps and therefore change the sequence of seeded forcing perturbations. No systematic numerical convergence study has established that the final geography is invariant to these controls. Passing the regression suite establishes the tested program behavior, not resolution-independent geology.

The omitted physics includes three-dimensional mantle flow, explicit slab geometry and tearing, temperature-dependent viscoplastic rheology, hydration and mineral phase changes, calibrated crustal mass transfer, climate-driven drainage and glaciation, ocean circulation, and water-volume-driven sea level. Statistical or visual plausibility should be distinguished from conservation accuracy, numerical convergence, and agreement with real geological observations. No 1,000 Myr accuracy claim is made.
