# Implementation gaps: things to address

Compiled 2026-09-22 against the current top-level source and the live run SEP21T
(`20260921-011257-b2f85b`, frames 0-102 Myr). Nothing in the engine or the run was changed.

**How this list was made.** Thirteen independent reviewers read the design documents,
five source subsystems, SEP21T's frames and Scotese's rules of thumb. A separate
whole-code audit from earlier the same day contributed nine more items. That gave
376 raw reports, merged into 122 distinct gaps. A completeness critic added 5 more
(ids C01-C05). An adversarial verifier then checked every item against the code the
native engine actually runs for SEP21T: **110 confirmed, 16 partly addressed, 1 not
a gap** (G105, left in its section for the record). All but two apply to the live run:
G11 affects only the legacy path, and G98 (flexure) exists as foreland loading but
`reviewed_v1` switches it off. 115 of the 127 items would change simulated behaviour and
need approval before being built. Each run freezes its own sources, so a fix reaches
SEP21T only through a recorded migration.

**Scope of references in this file.** Every `file:line` and section reference is to the
desktop installation's source at SEP21T 102 Myr, not to this branch. "PHYSICS_REPAIRS
section 18" is this branch's "Trench identity across plate splits and remnant absorption",
and G58 is already implemented here, by the `transfer_split` call in
`native_topology._retire_remnants`.

**Caveats.** Numbers come from saved frames of a run still in progress. Per-frame
diagnostics record only the last substep, so per-step ratios are samples, not
integrals. Fix directions are starting points from the reviewers, not designs. Ids
are stable handles for discussion; within each section items are ordered by impact,
then effort.

## Plate driving forces and motion

- **G03 — Slab Stokes drag and anchor term act as a hidden speed limit** _(high impact · effort M · partly addressed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* No plate ever exceeds about 3.4-4 cm/yr, the mean is about 1 cm/yr, and slab Stokes drag takes about half of all dissipation. Oceans close 3-5x slower than on Earth, and nothing moves like India or the fast Pacific (6-10+ cm/yr).
  - *Code does:* SLAB_STOKES_PA_S = 3e22 is scaled by slab length along with the slab weight, so a full slab has a terminal speed of roughly 2.6-4.7 cm/yr before any other resistance. The anchor term (another 3e22) raises upper-plate resistance from 0.71x to 1.71x. (`plate_balance.py:83-84`; `plate_balance.py:86-88`; `plate_balance.py:399-403`)
  - *Fix:* Make slab resistance a consequence of the slab's actual sinking rather than a length-proportional brake. Stokes drag on a sinking body should scale with its cross-section and the local viscosity.
  - *Already in progress:* PR-6 (open) aligns pull and resistance extents but does not remove the cap
- **G02 — Frozen continents: purely continental plates are nearly force-free** _(high impact · effort L · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Continental plates drift at about 0.4-1.7 cm/yr while the primordial oceans move about 3.0-3.4 cm/yr (frame_0044). A plate's speed tracks how much ocean it carries.
  - *Code does:* Ridge push counts only same-plate ocean cells. Slab pull reaches a plate only through its own incoming ocean or the moving-hinge overriding reaction. (`plate_balance.py:478`; `plate_balance.py:494`; `plate_balance.py:77`)
  - *Fix:* Give continents their force paths physically rather than tuning keels. (a) Transmit ocean-plate loads through the plate: once a continent's plate contains attached ocean lithosphere, the ridge GPE and slab pull on that ocean already act on the plate, so the fix is to make sure breakup and accretion leave continents attached to their ocean (see G01).
- **G05 — Stationary mantle: no slab-induced flow, trench suction or lower-mantle slab suction** _(high impact · effort L · partly addressed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Plates that do not touch a trench feel nothing from a nearby slab. Continents do not converge on subduction girdles or assemble over downwellings (Pangaea-style assembly does not emerge).
  - *Code does:* Basal drag acts against a motionless mantle. Only the upper-mantle part of a uniform slab column pulls. (`plate_balance.py:43`; `slab_memory.py:30-32`; `plate_balance.py:359-366`)
  - *Fix:* Add a low-order mantle-flow traction: treat each retained slab (its upper-mantle portion and its deeper conserved inventory) as a Stokeslet or line-sink density anomaly. Compute the induced horizontal mantle velocity at the base of the lithosphere with an analytic spherical-harmonic or Green's-function kernel.
- **G07 — No continental GPE boundary force in the plate balance** _(high impact · effort L · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* A thick orogen or plateau next to ocean, like an Andean margin, puts no force on either plate. Convergence is not slowed until two continental sheets actually overlap (1,404-1,640 overlap pairs at 90-94 Myr, all from collision stacks).
  - *Code does:* Ridge push uses only same-plate ocean gradients and zeroes the gradient on continents. Continental GPE reaches the balance only as the cross term of overlapping collision sheets. (`plate_balance.py:465-470`; `plate_balance.py:494`; `plate_balance.py:509`)
  - *Fix:* Treat lithospheric GPE as one depth-integrated body force: the gradient of the column's GPE per area across every column, ocean and continent alike, taken against the same mantle/asthenosphere reference, with the ocean part being today's ridge push. Continents would then feel force from their own relief contrast with the ocean around them, applied at the continent-ocean transition and across plate boundaries.
- **G09 — Boundary resistances are uniform constants with no material, thermal or strain-history dependence** _(high impact · effort L · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* An arc-arc or terrane-arc contact away from a trench costs 1e13 N/m, the same as a craton-craton collision, so small terranes and microplates lock at first contact instead of shearing past or underthrusting (SEP21T: 1-4 compression elements at yield per step, 756-781 strike-slip). Old sutures, fracture zones and abandoned rifts get no preference as new boundaries or as weak strike-slip zones.
  - *Code does:* Every closing non-trench edge costs COMPRESSIVE_N_PER_M = 1e13 N/m (a cold-craton value), whether arc-arc, ocean-ocean or craton-craton. Megathrust (1.8e12 N/m) and strike-slip (15 MPa) are also fixed. (`plate_balance.py:101`; `plate_balance.py:105`; `plate_balance.py:111`)
  - *Fix:* Make each boundary resistance an integral of the strength of the rock actually at the edge: a Byerlee/ductile envelope set by crust type, thickness and geotherm for both columns (the weaker governs), multiplied by the damage/weakness state enhanced_rifting already carries. Let accumulated slip or shear strain add damage that heals with time, stored on the material, not in the balance.
- **G01 — No path from slab/ridge forces to internal continental tension** _(high impact · effort XL · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Continents essentially never split. SEP21T's current frame_0044 records 2 breakup events by 88 Myr, and the first one took only about 0.15 Mkm2 of continent.
  - *Code does:* plate_balance solves only rigid Euler rotations, with no intraplate stress. Continental damage comes only from realized sheet stretching driven by boundary relative velocities. (`plate_balance.py:44`; `plate_balance.py:359-375`; `enhanced_rifting.py:253-258`)
  - *Fix:* Recover the internal traction field from the forces the balance already has. After plate_balance solves omega, the residual between each plate's applied line and body forces (slab W sin(dip) at its trench, ridge GPE gradient, basal drag and edge resistances) and its rigid motion is exactly the load the plate interior carries.
- **C03 — The net-rotation check tests only an upper bound, and the run sits 10-40x below Earth, so the drag calibration it is meant to pin is unverified** _(medium impact · effort S · confirmed · no physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Net rotation stays 10-40x below the observed 0.1-0.26 deg/Myr range. Mean plate speed is about 1.1 cm/yr and the fastest plate about 4.1 cm/yr at 100 Myr.
  - *Code does:* plate_balance says the asthenosphere drag coefficient (which sets every absolute speed) and the stiff-end slab Stokes/anchor viscosity are pinned by the net-rotation bound. The bound is only reported, never tested from below. (`plate_balance.py:69-72`; `plate_balance.py:80-82`; `plate_balance.py:136-140`)
  - *Fix:* Do not tune drag. Make the calibration two-sided and visible: report net rotation against both the 0.1 lower and 0.26 upper observed values, plus speeds of slab-attached and slab-free plates against Scotese's classes, and flag when the drag or Stokes argument is not satisfied.
- **G11 — Default fresh worlds use legacy lagged, noise-driven, speed-capped dynamics** _(medium impact · effort S · confirmed · physics change · legacy path only)_
  - *Seen:* No effect on SEP21T. A CLI or API world without an explicit profile moves by seeded noise with a 24 Myr lag and a 0.014 rad/Myr cap.
  - *Code does:* NATIVE_DEFAULTS sets physics_profile='legacy'. That law uses an outward insulation drift, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noise, a 24 Myr velocity lag, spin subtraction, a 0.014 rad/Myr cap and kinematic back-arc/transform corrections. (`native_engine.py:106`; `native_engine.py:114-115`; `raster_engine.py:377-381`)
  - *Fix:* Make the force-balanced profile the default for new native worlds, keeping legacy only for resuming old checkpoints. Default primordial subduction on so a reviewed world starts with inherited slabs, a physical initial condition in line with Scotese's rule that the world begins mid-cycle.
  - *Addressed 2026-09-27 (partly):* new reviewed worlds from the app, the API and the command line now select `thermal_v1` and enhanced rifting unless the request says otherwise, and write them explicitly into `config.json` (`fresh_world.fresh_world_request`, a server-only module outside the hashed engine sources; "Reviewed fresh-world law selection" in PHYSICS_REPAIRS.md). The app and the command line without `--config` default to `reviewed_v1`; a bare `POST /api/run {}` stays legacy, and a `--config` file is reproduced with its saved laws. The engine's missing-key default stays legacy on purpose, because branch re-validates saved configs and four saved reviewed runs lack the law keys. `moving_hinge_v1` is withheld as a default until the N1 back-arc repair, because it zeroes the retreat speed that back-arc nucleation needs. *Update 2026-09-28:* with N1 installed (#193), `moving_hinge_v1` is now also a fresh-world default. Primordial subduction stays an explicit initial-condition choice (open).
- **G10 — Microplate velocity spikes from inertia-free balance with area-scaled resistance** _(medium impact · effort M · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* At 92 Myr Rift plate 03 (0.27 to 0.19 Mkm2) jumped from 0.87 to 12.3 cm/yr mean, about 250 km in one 2 Myr step, spinning about 15 deg/Myr. It was back to 0.36 cm/yr at 94 Myr with reversed rotation.
  - *Code does:* Omega is solved fresh each step. Basal drag scales with footprint area, and edge resistance applies only to certain boundary types, so a tiny plate whose contact labels change has almost nothing resisting it. (`frame_0045/0046/0047.json`; `frame_0046.json`; `frame_0045->0046`)
  - *Fix:* Stay inertia-free but make the drivers and resistances physically smooth in the small-plate limit. (a) Integrate the collision cross-term torque from the actual overlap geometry so it cannot jump with discretization; the step-to-step jump in overlap pairs is a symptom.
- **G06 — No active mantle traction, plume push or plume lubrication** _(medium impact · effort L · partly addressed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* LIP eruptions leave plate speeds unchanged. There is no plume-head push, no acceleration of India-type plates after plume arrival, and no supercontinent-insulation uplift driving dispersal.
  - *Code does:* Asthenosphere drag only resists motion, with a keel factor keyed to crust class. The legacy seeded basal traction was deleted with no physical replacement, and LIP heat enters no force term. (`plate_balance.py:359-366`; `plate_balance.py:363`; `physics_profile.py:128`)
  - *Fix:* Represent plumes as fixed mantle upwellings. Make the LIP source a buoyant mantle anomaly that (a) contributes a radial-to-horizontal basal traction through the same mantle-flow kernel proposed for G05 (an upwelling source instead of a slab sink), and (b) thins the thermal lithosphere, so the keel/drag factor comes from a lithosphere-thickness field that LIP heat reduces.
- **G04 — slab_pull / ridge_push config values do nothing on the native path** _(low impact · effort S · confirmed · no physics change)_
  - *Seen:* SEP21T's ridge_push 0.25 changes nothing. The emergent slab/ridge work split is about 86/14 (slab 8.0e11 of 9.26e11 W).
  - *Code does:* Only the legacy raster velocity law reads the coefficients. The native balance always applies full ridge GPE and full slab weight. (`raster_engine.py:1347-1348`; `raster_engine.py:1298-1310`; `plate_balance.py:186,489`)
  - *Fix:* Do not re-introduce coefficient scaling, because the native balance derives both drivers physically. Either hide or disable the sliders when physics_profile is reviewed_v1, or re-map them to physical inputs that do change the balance (e.g. mantle temperature, which sets ridge GPE, or slab density contrast).
  - *Addressed 2026-09-23:* the two sliders are disabled and explained when `reviewed_v1` is selected, and stay live for the legacy law that reads them. Re-mapping them to physical inputs (mantle temperature, slab density contrast) remains open.

## Ocean floor bookkeeping and ridges

- **G39 — Ridge triple-junction cells get no new crust** _(high impact · effort M · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Every ridge-ridge-ridge or ridge-trench junction carries a plug of 25-70 Myr seafloor, instead of the youngest crust on the map, so each junction sits as a deep 'hole' at a ridge intersection. This spoils exactly the complex multi-plate junctions the user wants, such as microplate-bounded triple junctions and Galapagos- or Easter-style systems.
  - *Code does:* Crust is refused in any cell that belongs to or borders a third plate, and normalization fills it with stretched old crust. (`native_spreading.py:468-473`; `native_spreading.py:482`; `native_spreading.py:501`)
  - *Fix:* Replace the whole-cell foreign-neighbour veto with geometric clipping. Admit each pair's paired strip up to the third owner's actual contour, which already exists as a native boundary polygon, and let each of the three pairs fill its own sector of the junction cell.
- **G12 — Convergent ocean overlap is erased by support normalization instead of being subducted** _(high impact · effort L · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* About three quarters of closing ocean vanishes per step without feeding slabs or arcs. By 100 Myr, 112.8 Mkm2 of ocean had been created against 33.8 Mkm2 consumed, and mean ocean age is about 151 Myr, older than any real ocean.
  - *Code does:* native_subduction.removal captures only min(downgoing support, maturity-weighted swept polygon, local excess). The rest of the overlap is deleted by s.support = moved/total from both plates. (`native_processes.py:275`; `native_processes.py:327`; `native_subduction.py:250`)
  - *Fix:* Treat every closing overlap at a boundary with an incoming oceanic side as subduction by that trench's polarity. The captured polygon should be the full swept convergent area, not maturity-weighted, with trench maturity only affecting where the hinge sits.
- **G37 — New seafloor age averaged into control faces, so ridge crests read too old** _(high impact · effort L · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Mid-ocean ridges barely show on the 2 km/pixel map. Ridge pixels sit at about -4,450 m against about -2,600 m on Earth, with a median age of 29 Myr.
  - *Code does:* The newborn fraction is averaged into each 23,000-30,000 km2 cell (age = old x (1 - newborn) + dt/2 x newborn), so old crust is only diluted. (`native_processes.py:281`; `native_spreading.py:563`; `native_spreading.py:556`)
  - *Fix:* Stop representing the crest as a diluted cell mean. Carry a sub-cell age distribution in each ocean face, for example two or three age cohorts, or area-by-age bins transported as extra moments in advect_ocean, so newborn area keeps age ~dt/2 until transport moves it out.
- **G08 — Three inconsistent oceanic cooling models** _(medium impact · effort M · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* In GDH1 the seafloor is nearly flat beyond about 70 Myr: 100 Myr sits only about 150 m above the asymptote, where half-space would put it 3,650 m below the crest. The ridge-push law keeps a full age gradient out to 100 Myr and then drops to zero.
  - *Code does:* Bathymetry and slab mass use GDH1-style subsidence. Ridge push uses half-space cooling saturated at 100 Myr. (`slab_memory.py:81`; `native_frame_sampling.py:107`; `plate_balance.py:186-190`)
  - *Fix:* Carry one oceanic thermal state, a plate-cooling model that reproduces GDH1 depth, and derive everything from it: bathymetry, slab excess mass (already GDH1), ridge push as the horizontal gradient of that column's GPE, which then flattens naturally where the seafloor flattens, and thermal or elastic thickness for bending. This removes the separate 100 Myr cutoff.
- **G35 — Kinematic normal-motion gate decides where seafloor forms and where continents stretch** _(medium impact · effort M · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Slowly or obliquely opening boundaries (up to 2 km/Myr, or 15% of slip) are labelled transform and make no young crust; old ocean is stretched by normalization to fill the gap instead. There are no pull-apart basins, leaky transforms or ultraslow ridges, and slow continental rifts don't thin through the column path.
  - *Code does:* Seafloor forms, and continental stretch (bcode 5) feeds columns, only where opening > max(2 km/Myr, 0.15 x sliding). Below that the edge is 'transform' and the gap is filled by stretching old ocean through normalization. (`ridge_geometry.py:16-24`; `native_engine.py:462-469`; `native_spreading.py:239`)
  - *Fix:* Mirror the normal_partition treatment for extension. Admit any positive normal velocity to spreading and stretch in proportion to its opening component, and keep tangential traction for the slip part, giving continuous partitioning.
  - *Already in progress:* PR-2 (open, already reflected locally) unifies the threshold but retains it; PR-9 covers convergence only
- **G38 — Ridge flank asymmetry from support normalization; symmetry diagnostic is tautological** _(medium impact · effort M · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Ridges sit off-centre in their age pattern: the fast plate's flank gets the larger share of filled crust (reported 32/68). The diagnostic always reads perfectly symmetric, so the defect cannot be seen in frame JSON.
  - *Code does:* Opened gaps are closed by s.support = moved/total, which favours the fast plate. After the initial projection, ocean interfaces are finite-volume rather than an exact partition. side_p and side_q are appended from one duplicated area list. (`native_processes.py:275`; `native_spreading.py:545`; `native_spreading.py:541-543`)
  - *Fix:* Measure symmetry after normalization, not before. Compare each flank's actual owned young-crust area, or the age-distance profile, within about 300 km of the axis for each owner pair.
  - *Already in progress:* PHYSICS_REPAIRS.md ridge-ownership section (fixed the relabelling, not normalization); PR-2 test enshrines the tautology
- **G36 — No ridge-transform network or fracture-zone trails** _(medium impact · effort L · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Ridges are smooth or zig-zag mesh contours with no orthogonal transform offsets. Ocean age maps show no fracture-zone stripes, so you cannot read past plate motion directions or trench-pointing fracture zones (Rule III) off the seafloor.
  - *Code does:* Ridges are owner-support contours or mesh edges (including zig-zag initial ridges), and first-order upwind transport smears offsets. No segmentation or fracture-zone process exists. (`KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md:23`; `MESH.md:67`; `native_spreading.py:232-236`)
  - *Fix:* Orient ridge segments perpendicular to the local spreading direction, which is already computed per spreading piece. Wherever that axis must step to follow the plate contour, insert a transform edge parallel to relative motion.

## Subduction lifecycle

- **C04 — A 20 Myr young-age gate hides ridge-trench encounters, so slab windows never form** _(high impact · effort M · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Over 0-100 Myr there are no ridge-subduction events and no slab windows. No adakitic or slab-window volcanism or uplift appears on the overriding plate.
  - *Code does:* ridge_interaction only admits ridge edges whose two control cells are both 20 Myr old or younger, plus a trench-side downgoing cell of 20 Myr or younger. Crest ages are cell averages (new crust enters at dt/2, mixed into 160-240 km faces), and spreading is slow. (`ridge_interaction.py:17`; `ridge_interaction.py:50`; `ridge_interaction.py:62`)
  - *Fix:* Detect the encounter geometrically, not by age. A spreading-coded boundary (bcode 1, ocean-ocean, positive opening) whose axis comes within a physical distance of a subduction boundary of a third plate and is approaching counts, and the axis age is zero by definition.
- **G14 — Fixed 10 Myr / 100 km maturity clock throttles new slabs** _(high impact · effort M · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* frame_0044 has 17 mature and 34 initiating systems. Mature ones are mostly t=0 inheritances with maturity pinned to 1 (ids 3, 17, 30, 107, 110, 122, 141, each with 1,800-3,300 km of shortening).
  - *Code does:* maturity = min(1, active_myr/10, shortening/100 km) multiplies capture, arc supply, slab strength and retreat. A reactivated trench resets to 0 even if its slab mass remains. (`trench_history.py:34-35`; `trench_history.py:568-570`; `trench_history.py:551-553`)
  - *Fix:* Replace the clock with the state it stands in for. Take pull and capture weighting from the slab's own retained inventory, for example down-dip length or mass versus the ~100-200 km depth where slab pull becomes self-sustaining, using the slab_memory ledger (which already carries kg/m line density).
- **C05 — No along-strike slab tearing or STEP faults** _(high impact · effort L · partly addressed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Slabs never detach progressively along an orogen. The weight of a jammed segment shifts onto the rest of the chain, and the whole slab later stops at once through timers.
  - *Code does:* Each connected trench chain between two plates carries one slab inventory with a uniform down-dip column and a uniform line load. A chain shuts down as a whole once at least 50% of its length is collided or buoyant (after the 6 Myr timer). (`slab_memory.py:14-15`; `slab_memory.py:18-19`; `trench_history.py:599-600`)
  - *Fix:* Promote the existing local neck-history path (slab_tether_local, damage integrated per patch, rupture and conservative retirement) to production for new native worlds, once the continental-entry buoyancy it depends on is in. Tears then come from local damage where buoyant crust enters or the trench ends at a transform.
  - *Already in progress:* PHYSICS_REPAIRS.md 'Remaining review work: Local slab-neck history extension' and 'Coupled rupture and native source intervals' (experimental, not selected for live worlds)
- **G13 — Subduction initiation is instant and free at any closing boundary** _(high impact · effort L · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* frame_0044 events: 81 trench_initiating, 67 quiet, 29 resumed, 11 reactivated. Trenches 144, 146, 147 and 148 are 2 Myr old with 0-2 km of shortening, and 34 systems are 'initiating'.
  - *Code does:* Any edge with normal speed <0 (no minimum) and water on one side becomes a subduction candidate and gets an 'initiating' trench record that step. In the force solve it switches from 1e13 N/m compression to 1.8e12 N/m megathrust once any slab mass exists. (`normal_partition.py:26`; `native_engine.py:480-481`; `trench_history.py:455-457`)
  - *Fix:* Make initiation a failure criterion driven by the force balance the engine already solves. Accumulate resisted compressive work on each convergent edge: boundary-normal traction times shortening, fed from the plate_balance compression term.
- **G17 — Extension and quiet timers kill trenches under net divergence** _(high impact · effort L · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Most trench deaths in SEP21T are 'sustained_extension' (21 of 28). Trenches go quiet and resume repeatedly (67 quiet, 29 resumed, 11 reactivated).
  - *Code does:* A trench shuts down after 10 Myr with at least 50% extension or after 20 Myr quiet, and its pull is dropped even though inventory remains. The hinge moves with the upper plate, so upper-plate retreat reads as extension. (`trench_history.py:37-38`; `trench_history.py:596-602`; `trench_history.py:610-611`)
  - *Fix:* Treat the slab as its own body: give the hinge a rollback velocity from the retained slab's negative buoyancy (the slab_memory line load) against mantle drag. Measure trench activity as consumption relative to the hinge (plate velocity minus hinge velocity), not plate-pair closing speed.
- **G21 — Heuristic forearc-sweep rollback still runs under moving_hinge_v1** _(high impact · effort L · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* About 16 Mkm2 of downgoing ocean has been relabelled as overriding plate by 88 Myr, with no spreading axis or young crust. Retreat is fastest where convergence is fastest and capped at 0.8 cm/yr, the opposite of the Mariana (slow convergence, fast rollback) vs Andes pattern.
  - *Code does:* native _advance_step calls the legacy _migrate_trenches/extend_forearcs every step. It moves downgoing ocean to the overriding plate at min(8, 0.15 x convergence x buoyancy/(1+2.5 craton)) km/Myr. _boundaries zeroes trench_retreat_speed before the solve, so the moving-hinge guard never fires. (`native_engine.py:682`; `raster_engine.py:925`; `raster_engine.py:951-954`)
  - *Fix:* Remove the extend_forearcs membership sweep from the moving-hinge path and derive trench motion from forces instead (see G22): a trench degree of freedom driven by slab negative buoyancy against mantle resistance. Any upper-plate stretching should then open real back-arc ocean through the existing rift/spreading machinery.
  - *Addressed 2026-09-27:* the sweep's side effect on back-arcs is resolved without reviving the sweep.   - *Fix:* Replace the veto with strength. Cratons already carry a 6x resistance and a thick root.
- **G49 — Three-way (triple-junction) rift cuts are refused** _(medium impact · effort M · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* A fully failed Y-shaped damage belt is skipped every step. Because the failed edges stay failed, the same 3-way partition keeps coming back and keeps being refused.
  - *Code does:* Both commit paths skip any cut that does not give exactly two pieces, even when coherent_cut returns more. (`rift_mesh.py:266-274`; `progressive_rifting.py:650-652`; `ocean_rifting.py:198-199`)

- **G52 — Rift-scar inversion is a kinematic distance-kernel label** _(medium impact · effort M · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* The 36 rift_inversion events in 0-88 Myr record overlap between an 1,800 km distance kernel from convergent boundaries and old rift scars. They do not record reactivated faults.
  - *Code does:* An inversion request is built from convergent boundaries within 1,800 km (300 km contact scale, 2 km cap) and relabels min(request, realized uplift). (`rift_inversion.py:3-4`; `rift_inversion.py:17-22`; `structure_engine.py:266-273`)

- **G41 — Breakup is a discrete threshold cut, not progressive loss of connectivity** _(medium impact · effort L · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Continents part in a single step along the whole rift length, with no diachronous opening, V-shaped basin tip or failed arm (aulacogen) that got partly oceanised. The 2 Myr frames show a full-length ocean gap appearing at once.
  - *Code does:* A plate splits in one step once links pass damage >= 0.95, strain >= RUPTURE_STRAIN and opening > 0.02 km/Myr, plus viability checks. There is no partially ruptured front. (`progressive_rifting.py:51-53`; `progressive_rifting.py:630-631`; `progressive_rifting.py:651`)
  - *Fix:* Let failed links become a boundary segment before the cut is complete. A connected failed chain that ends inside the plate becomes a propagating rift tip: new ocean is admitted locally along the ruptured part, driven by realized opening, while the unruptured remainder stays a deforming continental belt (the existing deforming_regions sheet).
- **G44 — Rift strength is a dimensionless proxy with seeded noise, not thermal rheology** _(medium impact · effort L · partly addressed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Outside cratons, sutures and already-stretched belts, continental interiors have nearly uniform strength modulated by random noise (seed weakness on 75% of material parcels). Rifts nucleate where boundaries load the edges, rather than along hot, thin-lithosphere corridors.
  - *Code does:* Resistance = (1+5 craton)/(1+1.2 suture+heat/800+2.5 damage) x (1 - seed weakness), clipped to 0.12-12. It has no geotherm or lithosphere thickness. (`enhanced_rifting.py:175-182`; `progressive_rifting.py:124-128`; `progressive_rifting.py:137`)
  - *Fix:* Add one lithospheric-thermal state per material parcel: lithosphere thickness or Moho temperature, advected with the material, thinned by the realized strain (beta), reset by LIP/plume heat and thickened by cooling. Derive the resistance from it using an integrated-strength (yield-envelope) relation instead of the (1+5 craton)/(...) quotient.
- **G53 — Rift phases set by a fixed 20 Myr quiet timer and damage thresholds** _(low impact · effort S · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Rift systems flip to 'failed' exactly 20 Myr after strain falls below 1e-5 (17 events in SEP21T). From then on they stop seeding scar memory, so rifts that were quiet a little longer carry less inherited weakness for later inversion or reactivation.

Seed scars from the actual recorded extension whatever the label, so the label is diagnostic only.

## Plate topology and resolution

- **G55 — Plates never merge, after collision or across dead boundaries** _(high impact · effort L · confirmed · physics change)_

After the force solve, where the weld and contact elements carry the full relative motion (the plastic weld is not at capacity and relative velocity across every shared boundary is near zero within solver tolerance), with no ridge or trench on that boundary, the boundary has stopped being a boundary.

- **G54 — At most one topology change per step, in fixed priority order** _(medium impact · effort M · confirmed · physics change)_

- **G59 — Mesh-size floors block microplates; detached bodies ride distant parents** _(medium impact · effort M · partly addressed · physics change)_

- **G57 — Primordial ocean plates exempt from breakthrough splitting by identity** _(low impact · effort S · confirmed · physics change)_

## Collision, accretion and terranes

- **G68 — 70 Myr minimum plate age before any accretion** _(high impact · effort S · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Nothing can dock anywhere during the first 70 Myr of the world. Any plate born by rifting or splitting (for example rift plate 01, born about 74 Myr) cannot receive or give a terrane until it is 70 Myr old, and neither can its partner.
  - *Code does:* An accretion plan is skipped if either plate is younger than MIN_PLATE_AGE_MYR = 70. (`local_accretion.py:28`; `local_accretion.py:592-594`)
  - *Fix:* Delete the plate-age gate. Whatever it protects against (freshly split fragments re-merging immediately) should come from mechanics: a newly split pair is diverging, so it produces no closing contact and no locked suture.
- **G69 — Accretion loading ignores slow or oblique contacts** _(high impact · effort S · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* A strongly oblique margin (sliding more than about 2.9 times closing), or a slowly converging one under 2 km/Myr (20 km per 10 Myr), gets zero accretion loading even with overlap and a mature collision contact. Displaced terranes along transpressive margins, the Cordilleran/SE Asian style, cannot dock.
  - *Code does:* Observations are dropped unless closing speed > max(2 km/Myr, 0.35 x sliding). This is the known gap 5 threshold, here used as a transfer gate. (`local_accretion.py:420-423`; `local_accretion.py:484-486`; `PHYSICS_REPAIRS.md:270-272`)
  - *Fix:* Load the contact by its normal (closing) component through the existing strain-partition (normal_partition v1) at every contacting front, with no obliquity cutoff. Oblique motion then contributes its normal part to locking and its tangential part to margin-parallel translation of the arriving terrane (a strike-slip sliver), instead of cancelling accretion.
  - *Already in progress:* PR-2 (pull-requests/pr-2.json) aligns boundary classification at 0.15 but explicitly keeps local accretion's own gate; nothing addresses this gate
- **G63 — Collision stacking order set by first-contact surface height, not prior subduction polarity** _(high impact · effort M · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* When a subducting continent arrives at a trench, which sheet overrides can flip against the trench's polarity based on metre-scale height noise. The thickened root, foreland basin and plateau then form on the wrong side of the suture (an 'Asia under India' case).
  - *Code does:* admit_contact puts the higher sheet on top (by area-weighted height) and never rescores or consults trench polarity. (`collision_contacts.py:422`; `collision_contacts.py:428`; `collision_contacts.py:436`)
  - *Fix:* At admit_contact, look up the trench system on the contacting boundary in the ledger. If one exists, the historical downgoing plate's sheet becomes under_sheet, so polarity is inherited from subduction.
- **G66 — Consolidation blocked by a 4 Mkm2 rim and no-whole-body rule** _(high impact · effort M · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Buried, welded arc slivers and small terranes stay owned by the lower plate and are dragged back out or subducted with it. A donor sheet smaller than 4 Mkm2 (for example a microcontinent or terrane) can never be consolidated, because budget = area - 4e6 <= 0.
  - *Code does:* The donor must keep CONSOLIDATION_RIM_AREA_KM2 = 4e6, no connected donor body may move whole, and at most 2.5e6 km2 moves per step. Contained sheets have no front direction. (`collision_contacts.py:69`; `collision_contacts.py:77`; `collision_contacts.py:158-170`)
  - *Fix:* Drop the fixed rim and the whole-body ban. Leave a buried, welded face with the lower plate only if it carries the gravitational driver, meaning faces that still have an exposed free edge or a live trench or slab tether computed from the contact geometry.
- **G79 — Control cells count as continental only above 50% cover, so small terranes are invisible** _(high impact · effort M · partly addressed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* A sub-cell terrane or arc (under about 12,500 km2 of exposed area in a cell of about 25,000 km2) reaching a trench is still labelled ocean-ocean subduction. It gets no collision boundary code and no collision-edge shortening or uplift in deform_and_accrete.
  - *Code does:* A cell is crust only if at least 50% of its ~25,000 km2 is exposed material. Collision codes and polarity use this label, and stall triggers only when no water remains. (`native_engine.py:408`; `normal_partition.py:36-39`; `native_processes.py:355,362-363`)
  - *Fix:* Classify collision per boundary segment from the exact material geometry already used by edge_ocean_fraction and collision_contacts, for example the buoyant-material length fraction on the incoming side. Drive the collision shortening and uplift from the measured material overlap-area rate rather than from the control-cell label.
- **G67 — Terrane docking needs every root engaged within 400 km and mature for 18 Myr** _(high impact · effort L · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* From 0 to 100 Myr only about 1,266 km2 accreted in total, a few microscopic slivers (about 18 x 18 km each at 2 km/px) out of 141 collision contacts. Large arcs and microcontinents touching continents stay separate plates indefinitely: 787-root and 494-root bodies had 45 and 22 roots engaged and none mature.
  - *Code does:* Only a whole connected component with every root inside the 400 km zone and mature (18 process-weighted Myr, 35 Myr memory) can transfer. This amounts to a hidden size cap, and the code calls it 'a parameterized accretion heuristic'. (`native_engine.py:296`; `native_processes.py:454-455`; `localized_accretion.py:6-9`)
  - *Fix:* Replace whole-component, all-roots-mature qualification with a mechanical detachment criterion. The suture's locked shear/normal traction (already assembled by plate_balance/collision_contacts weld rows) should be compared with the strength of the weakest connected cut through the arriving body (for example, the thinnest or most-damaged rift or backarc lineage).
- **G56 — Oceanic lithosphere has no mechanical material column** _(high impact · effort XL · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Intra-oceanic overriding plates (Izu-Bonin, Tonga type) and oceanic plateau docking cannot come from ocean lithosphere itself. Ocean subduction polarity relies on a hard override.
  - *Code does:* The ocean is a fractional ownership and age field on a fixed mesh, and only continental and arc crust are Lagrangian. The docs say this coupling must exist before polarity overrides can be removed. (`native_material_adaptivity.py:1`; `native_processes.py:209-214`; `PHYSICS_REPAIRS.md:483-485`)
  - *Fix:* Give oceanic lithosphere a thin Lagrangian column wherever it matters mechanically: near trenches and on thickened plateau or LIP crust. Reuse the parcel machinery with an ocean crust type so the overriding plate and buoyant plateaus carry thickness and density into the contact and collision solves.
- **G75 — No escape or extrusion tectonics** _(high impact · effort XL · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Collisions only thicken and stack crust, with combined columns up to about 100 km and pair stacks near 149 km. No Anatolia- or Indochina-style block is ever expelled along a strike-slip fault, so indentation never produces new microplates or pull-apart basins.

  - *Fix:* Add a stress-driven failure path for shear and compression that mirrors the tensile rift path. Compute the deviatoric stress (or strain-rate and damage) that the viscous sheet already carries near collision fronts.
- **G65 — Suture-weld consolidation transfers single faces (one-triangle islands)** _(medium impact · effort M · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* When consolidation does fire, scattered single triangles change owner, leaving one-triangle islands of the upper plate inside the lower plate, and small faces jump tens of km in a step. Currently latent in SEP21T.
  - *Code does:* Faces are ranked one by one by exposure and burial, cut to a budget, then released individually to keep connectivity. (`collision_contacts.py:763-766`; `collision_contacts.py:767`; `collision_contacts.py:773-774`)
  - *Fix:* Select transfers as connected components: flood-fill the buried, welded candidate faces into patches, rank whole patches by mean burial, and admit or withhold them as units within the area budget, with connectivity checked per patch. A terrane then joins the upper plate as one coherent block.
- **G70 — Accretion transfer rules: smaller body moves, one per step, arcs relabelled on docking** _(medium impact · effort M · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* When a small forearc or upper-plate sliver meets a larger downgoing body, the sliver is handed to the downgoing plate, so terranes can travel the wrong way into the subduction side. Two docking events in one 2 Myr step are serialized, and the losing one waits and decays by exp(-dt/35).
  - *Code does:* The source is whichever component is smaller, regardless of polarity. At most one plan executes per step worldwide, and kind 3 faces become kind 1 at transfer. (`local_accretion.py:595`; `local_accretion.py:629`; `native_processes.py:454`)
  - *Fix:* Choose the transferring body by collision polarity: material on the under_owner side at the locked front, the lower-plate or scraped block, moves to the top_owner. Let every independent qualifying plan in disjoint regions execute in the same step, and serialize only plans that share material or plates.
  - *Addressed 2026-09-24:* N4, terranes ping-ponging between plates ("Welded stacks are one accretion body" in PHYSICS_REPAIRS.md). Under `accretion_welded_stack_version = 1`, which fresh reviewed native worlds select, sheets that overlap on one plate (collision_contacts' 'accreted' pairs, by more than the 1 km2 unordered-overlap floor of §17) form one accretion body. The size rule now compares whole welded stacks and a docked terrane can no longer be torn back out of its host's stack. Welding memory keyed on the source plate is released for rock that has left it in an accretion transfer (not yet for consolidation, trench-split transfer or remnant absorption). SEP21T and saved checkpoints keep policy 0. The polarity choice and one-plan-per-step parts of G70 remain open; with welded stacks the unresolved polarity choice now moves whole stacks (up to about 300 km deep) rather than 25 km slivers. The alternating block 02 / Rift plate 01 exchange of nine different arcs (176-206 Myr) is a front-geometry and polarity matter and is largely unchanged. *Follow-up 2026-09-27:* sub-1 km2 same-plate slivers no longer weld (a 0.024 km2 roundoff contact had frozen a dockable stack).
- **G71 — Suture and weld strength follow fixed clocks, not thermal state** _(medium impact · effort M · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Every suture strengthens and weakens on the same 50/150 Myr schedule, whatever its thermal history or composition. A contact with about 300 km of closure over a sub-pixel overlap counts as a fully mature suture.
  - *Code does:* suture_strength = 1 - exp(-convergence/100 km). Weld capacity goes from 1.5e12 to 1e13 on a 50 Myr clock, and cohesion anneals toward 0.25 on a 150 Myr clock. (`collision_contacts.py:48-50`; `collision_contacts.py:55-61`; `collision_contacts.py:257-259`)
  - *Fix:* Drive weld capacity from the retained thermal inventory the model already carries (thermal_v1, retained dense crust temperatures). Map suture-zone Moho temperature to a strength envelope between the hot and cold bounds instead of an idle-time exponential.
- **G72 — Dense underthrust continental roots exert no pull at the suture** _(medium impact · effort M · partly addressed · physics change)_

  - *Already in progress:* PR-10 and PR-11 (density-aware column gravity, retained dense crust) are applied in source as the column-GPE effect; no PR adds continental slab pull
- **G81 — Arc emplacement overlaps get no layer order** _(medium impact · effort M · confirmed · physics change)_

- **G64 — Collision interface uses borrowed Newtonian rheology with no yield** _(medium impact · effort L · confirmed · physics change)_

  - *Seen:* Collision uplift comes as blocks the size of birth-root lineage groups (10,061 groups). All faces in a root/sheet group get the same support value, so the uplift sits on a plateau instead of forming a flexural foreland/hinterland profile.

- **G76 — No accretionary prisms or offscraped sediment** _(medium impact · effort L · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Trench-facing margins show an arc and nothing seaward of it: no outer-arc ridges, coastal ranges or accretionary complexes like Makran or the Franciscan. Consumed ocean and eroded sediment simply disappear, so long-lived subduction margins do not grow outward.

  - *Fix:* Carry a sediment/thickness field on ocean faces, fed by the eroded volume that already leaves the column budget and by pelagic accumulation with age. - **G77 — No ophiolite obduction** _(medium impact · effort L · confirmed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* When a back-arc or small basin closes, the suture carries no belt of obducted oceanic crust. Vanished oceans leave only a thickened seam between continents, and nothing on the map records Scotese Rule VIII.
  - *Code does:* Ocean is an Eulerian field that cannot be thrust, and emplacement is explicitly not solved. (`SCIENCE.md:178`; `RULES-OF-THUMB.md:92`; `native_subduction.py:1-5`)

- **G74 — Collision fronts get one normal, a 2,000 km length cap and 4-point exposure sampling** _(low impact · effort M · partly addressed · physics change)_
  - *Seen:* Around a connected indenter corner, shortening is pushed along one averaged direction instead of fanning out, so the oroclinal bending and radial thrust vergence of a syntaxis cannot form. An active front longer than 2,000 km is loaded as 2,000 km.
  - *Code does:* Each overlap patch has one normal, front length is clamped to 1-2,000 km, and exposure uses 4 samples per face. (`collision_fronts.py:98-155`; `collision_contacts.py:501-503`; `collision_contacts.py:639-651`)
  - *Fix:* Subdivide each connected front along strike wherever the free-edge normal turns by more than the angle the mesh resolves (curvature-based segmentation from the clipped edges already computed), and give each segment its own normal and length. Remove the 2,000 km clamp: once segmentation bounds each piece's length, the clamp is unnecessary.

  - *Code does:* Offending faces are frozen to rigid motion and recomputed, 'a conservative nonpenetration approximation'. (`native_material_evolution.py:149-162`; `native_material_evolution.py:283-296`; `PHASE-ADJUSTED-COLUMN-FLOOR.md:159-162`)
